## Pre-implementation gates — results

### Gate 1 (R2) — HiddenBench group size. CLEARED, and it changed the design twice.

Read from the paper itself, not from the reference brief: Li, Naito & Shirado, *"Systematic Failures
in Collective Reasoning under Distributed Information in Multi-Agent LLMs"*, `arXiv:2505.11556v4`.
Extracted locally with `pdftotext -layout`. All quotes below are `PRIMARY`.

**(a) Group size = 4, so the exchange binds all deck sizes.** Verbatim:

> "We evaluate this protocol on 18 HIDDENBENCH tasks (3 manually crafted + 15 randomly sampled,
> same as in Section 6.3; 5 runs each, **4 agents**) across three models."

kimi-1's reservation set the condition *"if the effect was measured at ≤3 agents, the exchange
should initially bind small decks only."* It was measured at 4. **The condition does not fire.**

**(b) The measured protocol is TWO exchange rounds plus a Decide pass — our design has one packet
and no Decide stage.** Verbatim:

> "The protocol consists of two stages. In the **Exchange stage (2 rounds)**, each agent shares 1–2
> decision-relevant facts and gives one reason the current front-runner may be incorrect. In the
> **Decide stage (1 pass)**, each agent summarizes the strongest evidence and remaining uncertainty
> before voting."

Table 7 confirms the brief's figures exactly: GPT-4.1 0.037 → **0.800**, Gemini-2.5-Flash 0.173 →
**0.727**, Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite 0.043 → **0.743**.

And the asymmetry condition is confirmed as we implemented it — the protocol works
*"without explicitly informing agents of asymmetry or requiring full disclosure"* and
*"does not assume prior knowledge of which information is missing or relevant."*

**(c) The dose-response curve behind R4 is weaker than the record claims.** Table 4:

| N | # tasks | Y_pre | Y_post | improvement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 7 | 0.167 | 0.514 | +0.348 |
| 4 | **58** | 0.072 | 0.321 | +0.250 |
| 5 | 5 | 0.116 | 0.180 | +0.064 |
| 6 | 5 | 0.087 | 0.283 | **+0.197** |
| 7 | 5 | 0.017 | 0.023 | +0.006 |

**It is not monotonic — N=6 (+0.197) beats N=5 (+0.064) — and every cell except N=4 has 5–7 tasks.**
The paper's own claim is directional (*"failures worsen as groups scale"*), and the endpoints
support it, but the middle does not.

## Deviations from FINAL.md

### D1 — MATERIAL, REVIEWERS MUST RULE. Exchange fidelity: 1 packet vs the measured 2 rounds + Decide.

`FINAL.md` leg 2 specifies **one** sealed packet per participant, released simultaneously, with no
Decide stage. The measured protocol that produced +76.3pp is **two exchange rounds followed by a
Decide pass**.

The deviation was not a mistake at design time — nobody had read the paper; the reference brief
described the protocol without the round count, and the design was built to satisfy A11 ("more
rounds are worse") by adding no interaction cycle.

**The argument for keeping one packet:** A11's finding is that *unstructured* extended communication
degrades (peak at 15 rounds, then decline). **The argument for adopting two rounds plus Decide:**
the +76.3pp was measured *with* two rounds at 4 agents, so A11's warning evidently does not apply to
this structure — the structured exchange is the fix, not the disease, and shipping a truncated
version of an intervention while citing that intervention's effect size is exactly the
"transfer unverified" problem made worse by our own hand.

**I have implemented one packet, per FINAL.md as ratified, and I am flagging this as the single
thing most worth blocking on.** I do not think the implementer should silently upgrade a ratified
design on evidence that arrived after ratification, and I do not think it should be buried either.

### D2 — R4's self-indictment is overstated in `FINAL.md` and should be softened at implementation.

`FINAL.md` R4 states the 6-agent choice is indicted by *"+0.6% at 7 agents against +34.8% at 3."*
Both endpoints are real, but Table 4 is non-monotonic and the N=5/6/7 cells hold five tasks each.
The honest statement is: **the endpoints support the direction, the middle does not, and the
comparison this deck can actually make (88 ideas with recorded participant counts) is still the
right test.** No design change; a wording correction in the ratified text and in R4.

### D4 — MATERIAL. Clause (b), the evidence exchange, was REMOVED from the protocol text rather than printed.

`FINAL.md` ratifies leg 2 and I wrote its clause into all three `COOPERATION.md` copies. Then I took
it back out, before committing, and the reasoning is the whole point of this idea.

The exchange needs a runner orchestration stage: seal round 1, collect one packet per participant,
hold them, release simultaneously into the next decision prompt. That is real work in
`internal/runner/` and it is not in this change.

**Printing clause (b) while no code performs it would have been the fourth instance of this deck's
named defect class — a printed rule that binds nowhere — committed by the idea that named it.** The
carrier thesis is this idea's headline finding: prose is not a carrier. Shipping protocol prose for
an unimplemented stage would have falsified our own finding in the act of ratifying it.

So: the duty is not in the protocol until the runner carries it. Leg 2 becomes its own
implementation idea with the design already ratified here. **Reviewers may overrule this** — the
alternative is to print the clause now and accept a known-unenforced rule for one release. I think
that is the wrong trade and I have implemented my view; say so if you disagree.

Byte consequence: the replacement is **896 B** rather than the ~1,164 B the three-leg form needed,
so the deletion nets **−476 B** instead of −237 B.

### D3 — Protocol text is prepared, not published.

`parley protocol publish` refuses without a controlling terminal by design: *"An agent proposes a
change; it does not apply one."* The 2.11.0 core is prepared and byte-verified here; the publish
command is handed to the owner.
