## Summary

A3 claims the §15.6 trigger cannot be *repaired* as a trigger — its three conjunctive conditions are each independently miscalibrated, and even a perfect condition set would be adjudicated at the wrong time (consensus-close, after anchoring) by the wrong medium (COOPERATION.md prose that no enforced prompt ever delivers). The deck's own code contains a natural experiment proving this: rules carried in the Go prompt templates plus validators (Refutation attempts, severity vocabulary, `reviewed-commit`) reach near-universal compliance, while equivalent rules left as protocol prose (`## Adversarial alternative`: 5 of 89 ideas; Phase 6 address-every-reviewer: ~7% per the brief) run at single digits. My proposal deletes §15.6's conditions entirely, moves its surviving substance into an unconditional round-1 template section (`## Existing alternatives`, null-result form allowed — already ratified in §15.6(a)), carries it through the same prompt+validator machinery the review half already uses, and pays for it with a net −726 B of shared rule text.

## Proposed approach

### 1. Diagnosis: three conditions, each wrong, conjunctively worse

§15.6's current trigger [PRIMARY: `parley-deck/COOPERATION.md:1348-1350`, quoted: "On `standard` and `deliberation`, if round 1 closes with no substantive disagreement and the idea's output is primarily a judgment rather than a mechanically decidable artifact, consensus MUST NOT close until…"]:

1. **"no substantive disagreement" is the within-frame loophole.** The condition tests for the *presence* of disagreement, not *structural divergence between frames*. Six agents arguing MAJOR-vs-MINOR severity inside one shared frame are "substantively disagreeing" by any natural reading, and the rule switches off exactly when the frame itself is uncontested. Disagreement volume is a measure of noise in the convergence process, not of diversity in the candidate space.
2. **"primarily a judgment rather than a mechanically decidable artifact" is backwards.** The class of task the critic identified (build/bundling/deploy mechanics) is mechanically decidable, and it is precisely where dominant off-the-shelf substitutes live — `pnpm deploy`, Proxmox Backup Server's native S3 datastore. Verification cannot catch a superior option that was never generated: tests test the thing you built, never the thing you didn't. Generation bias does not correlate with verifiability, so the judgment/artifact clause filters out the exact tasks the rule is for.
3. **§15.7 turns the rule off on `fast`** [PRIMARY: `COOPERATION.md:1379`, row quoted: "15.6 correlated agreement | no | yes (section in an existing round-02 file) | yes (assigned round artifact)"] — while B2-class tasks (small, reversible, mechanically verifiable) are exactly what the classifier routes *to* `fast`.

And the **timing is structural**: even when all three conditions pass, the alternative is steelmanned at consensus-close — after every participant has read and responded to the others' proposals. Design-fixation research shows exposure to a solution example constrains subsequent design to the example's features *including its flaws* [PRIMARY: Jansson & Smith 1991, "Design Fixation", *Design Studies* 12(1):3–11, DOI 10.1016/0142-694X(91)90011-F; definition located this session: "a blind adherence to a set of ideas or concepts limiting the output of conceptual design"; experiment groups shown an example solution reproduced its features and its faults versus controls]. A late steelman is a *recorded* alternative, not an *adopted* one — B1 demonstrates the difference.

### 3. The repair

**P1 — Trigger: delete the conditions, don't tune them.** No conjunction of agent-adjudicated semantic conditions ("substantive", "primarily a judgment") can fire reliably — each is adjudicated by the very agents whose convergence it is meant to police, and the deck's own §15.3 already distrusts agent-adjudicated resolution. Replace with a *syntactic* default: the section exists the way `## Risks` exists. Nobody adjudicates whether risks were likely enough to warrant a Risks section. The default-effect literature is unambiguous that opt-in structures collapse to near-zero adoption regardless of merit: presumed-consent (opt-out) Austria shows ~99.98% effective donation consent versus opt-in Germany's ~12% [PRIMARY: Johnson & Goldstein 2003, "Do Defaults Save Lives?", *Science* 302(5649):1338–1339, DOI 10.1126/science.1091721; central figures located via the DOI record this session, full text not opened]; automatic enrollment raises 401(k) participation dramatically versus opt-in [PRIMARY: Madrian & Shea, "The Power of Suggestion: Inertia in 401(k) Participation and Savings Behavior", NBER WP 7682 / *QJE* 116(4), 2001; abstract located and quoted this session: "401(k) participation is significantly higher under automatic enrollment"; the specific 37%→86% new-hire figure is RECALL — in the paper, not in the fetched abstract]. A conditional trigger is a default of OFF. This is not an agent-behavior problem to be prompted around; it is choice architecture.

**P2 — Timing: fire at round-1 generation, not consensus adjudication.** Independent written generation *before* interaction is the one manipulation that reliably increases the quantity and originality of candidate ideas: nominal and Delphi groups "are equally effective and both clearly are more effective than conventional interacting groups", which suppress ideas through conformity pressure and production blocking [PRIMARY: Van de Ven & Delbecq 1974, "The Effectiveness of Nominal, Delphi, and Interacting Group Decision Making Processes", *Academy of Management Journal* 17(4):605–621, DOI 10.5465/255641; quoted from the AOM abstract located this session]. The protocol already has the required isolation (Phase 1 round-1 independence discipline) — §15.6 simply demands its output at the end of the funnel instead of the start. The section therefore goes into the round-1 file, written before anyone reads anyone else's file, where an alternative can enter *as a candidate* rather than as a steelman of a rejected thing. This is also why debate-style convergence can't substitute: multi-agent debate systems "do not reliably outperform other proposed prompting strategies" and their dissent tends to be shallow [PRIMARY: Smit, Duckworth, Grinsztajn, Barrett, Pretorius, "Should we be going MAD? A Look at Multi-Agent Debate Strategies for LLMs", arXiv:2311.17371, abstract located and quoted this session], and sycophancy — adopting the interlocutor's position — "is a general behavior of state-of-the-art AI assistants" [PRIMARY: Sharma et al., "Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models", arXiv:2310.13548, abstract located and quoted this session]. Round 2+ amendability is the disease; the only cure that fits this deck is round-1 carriage.

**P3 — Carrier: ship it through the machinery the review half already proved.** Concretely, four diffs and no new concepts:

1. `internal/runner/runner.go:821` BuildRoundOnePrompt: required file shape gains `## Existing alternatives`; three lines of instruction, lifted verbatim in substance from §15.6(a)'s already-ratified null-result form — "Name the closest existing or off-the-shelf solution and the cheapest documented route the toolchain itself provides; adopt it or say why not. If none is found, record the search scope and what you checked — that is a finding, not a failure to comply." The null-result form is the intelligence-analysis tradecraft of ACH: the hypotheses list is generated *before* evaluation, and analysts are documented to otherwise settle on the first plausible hypothesis [the confirmation-tendency basis is PRIMARY: Davis's introduction to Heuer's *Psychology of Intelligence Analysis* (CIA CSI, 1999), located this session, quoted: "the tendency to see information confirming an already-held judgment more vividly than one sees 'disconfirming' information"; the Chapter 8 ACH characterization is SECONDARY: book and chapter located, chapter text not opened this session].
2. Validation reuses the exported `protocol.HasNonEmptySection` [PRIMARY: `internal/protocol/reviewartifact.go`, the same function the review gate uses] — no new validator concept, one call site.
3. `internal/app/driver_consensus.go:112` buildConsensusDraftPrompt: add the §15.6(b) sentence (unanimity among related models is a shared prior, and what would have to be true for the agreed position to be wrong). Better than prompt bytes: put it in the consensus *scaffold* as a heading, which is structural and cannot drift.
4. `skills/parley-deck/SKILL.md`: same section in the hand-driven template. The skill currently carries zero §15 content and its review template omits `## Refutation attempts` while the Go gate rejects artifacts lacking it [PRIMARY: read this session, `~/.zcode/skills/parley-deck/SKILL.md` lines ~760–790; grep for `15\.|PRIMARY|RECALL|steelman` → 0 hits] — the documented path emits artifacts the enforced path refuses. Longer-term (open question, not proposal): generate the hand-driven templates from the same Go source that builds the enforced prompts, the way buildFinalDraftPrompt already generates from `RequiredFinalSections`, so there is one prompt, not two.

### 6. What A3 does on B1 and on B2

**B1 — the missing option (PBS S3 datastore found in round 2, FINAL shipped the round-1 design anyway): partially addressed, and only via timing.** An unconditional round-1 `## Existing alternatives` section asks, before FINAL freezes and before cross-review anchoring, exactly the question that took a human-prompted round-2 discovery to ask — and Proxmox Backup Server's S3 backend is *documented toolchain behavior*, squarely inside "the cheapest documented route the toolchain itself provides". Moved to round 1, the option arrives as a candidate while adoption is still possible. But the actual B1 history — an alternative that *exists*, *wins its argument*, and still loses to the frozen frame — is an absorption failure, not a generation failure, and my axis deliberately does not touch it: a reframe found in round 2+ still has no finding class, route, or destination. That is A2's axis, and A3 is incomplete without it. I say this plainly rather than claiming B1 as a win.

**B2 — the unproposed option (`pnpm deploy` never on the table): directly addressed, with an honest bound.** The section converts a knowledge question ("does a simpler route exist?") into a lookup instruction ("what does the toolchain itself document?") — `pnpm --help` lists `deploy`; nobody has to *know* it, only to *look*. And the null-result form creates an auditable artifact: in the critic's case, a round-1 file recording "searched X, Y, Z; nothing simpler" is attackable in cross-review, whereas an unexamined absence cannot be cross-examined at all. The bound: a template heading forces attention, not knowledge. If the agent looks at `pnpm --help` and still doesn't connect "deploy" to the task, the section records a confident null. The mechanism raises the arrival rate of the cheap option; it does not guarantee it.

## Concerns / open questions

**Framing disclosure.** I notice myself largely accepting the brief's framing — the diagnosis-then-gap structure, the B1/B2 benchmarks, the "generation vs verification" vocabulary — and I derived my section text *while looking at both anchors*, which means "check the toolchain's documented route" fits B2 suspiciously well. That is one participant overfitting to two anecdotes, and the round should discount it accordingly. Two attacks on the framing itself: (1) The brief frames the problem as needing a *new generation mechanism*; the deck's own data equally supports an *enforcement* reading — §15.6 was ratified and then never delivered to a single enforced prompt, so "design it away" is premature until the ratified rule is actually shipped and measured. My proposal spends most of its force on delivery precisely because of this; it changes the trigger mostly because the trigger is what makes delivery pointless. (2) B1 and B2 are n=2 from one critic's memory [SECONDARY: quoted in the brief; the `servers/…/daily-backup-str` deck is outside this repository and I did not verify it]. A benchmark of two can select a mechanism that overfits both stories. My justification rests on the deck's measured non-carriage and the external defaults/fixation literature, not on the two cases — but the *section wording* does not have that independence, and I have no way to de-bias it alone.

**Where my axis fails.**
1. *Uniform blindness.* Default-on raises the arrival rate of the missed option but cannot beat a shared blind spot: if all participants share the model family's ignorance, all sections record the same compliant null, and the protocol observes success. §15.6(b) labels the unanimity a shared prior, which is honest but not corrective. Correlated priors produce correlated null-results — this is the residual core of the critic's complaint, and no template fixes it.
2. *Rubbish compliance.* "Checked; nothing simpler" is two lines away from being boilerplate. Requiring the search scope to name what was consulted (a docs page, a `--help` output) makes emptiness auditable, but that is exactly where ceremony pressure grows; §15.2's PRIMARY-with-locator discipline is the existing cultural enforcement, and it runs at whatever compliance §15 carriage gives it — which is currently zero.
3. *B1's absorption half* — not mine, as stated above; a proposal that claimed it would be claiming A2's work.
4. *Defaults cut both ways.* Johnson & Goldstein is an argument that each default-on must justify itself, not that everything should be default-on — which is why my table flips exactly two mechanisms and refuses a new flag.

**Defect target: A4-adversarial-appointment.** If forced to abandon A3, I defect there, not to A2. My axis makes *everyone* ask the question once, cheaply, by default; A4 makes *one* agent responsible for asking it repeatedly and adversarially, ideally model-diverse. The failure mode my axis cannot survive — uniform template, shared blind spot, correlated null-results — is exactly the one an appointed frame-breaker with defined success criteria attacks, because appointment transfers initiative, which defaults cannot. A2 is the correct *completion* of the story for B1 specifically (alternatives need a destination once they exist), but as a standalone it only builds a court for alternatives that something else must first generate; A4 is the something-else I would bet on if my carrier were removed.

**Open questions.**
- Should the hand-driven SKILL.md templates be *generated* from the Go prompt builders (single source of truth), rather than patched in parallel? This is the structural fix for the documented-vs-enforced divergence the deck has already recorded twice; it is tooling, not protocol bytes, but it changes how every future rule ships.
- Does `## Existing alternatives` belong in §15 (verification integrity) at all, once it fires at generation time? Its subject is candidate generation, not verdict validity; §4 Phase 1 may be its correct home, which would also shrink §15.
- The 7% Phase 6 figure and the 8-of-10 human-sourced frame reversals are taken from the brief [SECONDARY] — if round 2 relies on them, someone should re-measure them as PRIMARY; both are load-bearing for the carrier thesis and the A4-defect argument respectively.

## Risks

1. **Ceremony accretion via the null-result form.** If the search-scope requirement is tightened each time a null turns out to be lazy, the section grows into the next `require_model_diversity`-class dead ritual. Bound it: the scope names what was consulted, nothing more; no format, no minimum count.
2. **Validator brittleness.** `HasNonEmptySection` enforces presence, not quality — a one-word section passes. That is by design (the same trade the review gate already makes for `## Refutation attempts`), but it means the gate's ceiling is "the question was confronted", and over-claiming it would be the rubbish-compliance risk dressed as success.
3. **`fast`-track burden.** Adding a section to trivially small ideas is a real cost paid on every idea to catch a rare failure. Defense: 2–4 lines, null-form allowed, and B2-class tasks are *defined* into `fast` by the classifier — exempting `fast` would re-exclude the exact target class, which is the current bug, not a safeguard.
4. **Template drift.** Until the skill templates are generated from the Go source, every wording change must land in two places; the deck has already shipped one instance of the documented path emitting artifacts the enforced path rejects.
5. **Measurement risk.** The adoption and carriage numbers are from today's snapshot of this deck (89 ideas); if the corpus grows or the dead flags get cleaned up, the 0–5% framing should be re-measured before round 2 leans on it. My core claims (§15.6 text, prompt contents, zero Go enforcement, byte accounting) are file-located and re-checkable.
