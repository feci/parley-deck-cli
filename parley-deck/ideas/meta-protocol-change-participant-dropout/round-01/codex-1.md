## Summary

Broaden the shipped quota machinery with a versioned participant-failure trigger, rather than another membership system. Give every eligible non-protected dispatched step exactly one relaunch, then drop that participant for the lifetime of the idea if both attempts fail and the whole-batch floor holds. Preserve the old quota predicate for already-recorded 1.51.0 ideas; it must not silently become a broader policy on upgrade.

## Proposed approach

1. Keep `quota_auto_exclude` as the compatibility spelling at machine, deck, idea and CLI layers. New ideas freeze a versioned trigger such as `participant-failure-v1` alongside the saved enabled/scope fields. An absent trigger is exactly today's quota-only v1 behavior; serialize it omitted so historical hashes remain stable. Strictly reject unknown or malformed trigger values. No new knob is needed. Per-idea false opts out. Existing 1.51.0 ideas keep their predicate and scope; widening requires an explicit owner-bound revision through the existing mechanism.
2. The new predicate observes a dispatched step's terminal outcome from the supervisor and the shared artifact validator. It covers nonzero exit, command start failure, crash, provider failure regardless of code, configured timeout/watchdog, and exit zero with missing/malformed/invalid expected output. It never treats ❌, reservations, findings or disagreement as invalidity. A valid completed artifact or later successful attempt wins. Error text is supporting evidence, never authority by itself. Integrity/budget/policy/authority refusals and explicit operator cancellation stop the run rather than becoming removable participant failures: no authorized dispatched step has failed in those cases. A true child-process crash/timeout still counts.
3. Use exactly two attempts total, not two new retries on top of the current first-output watchdog retry. Reuse the attempt loop for exec and ACP, with fresh invocation IDs and retry linkage, no session resumption, and one small fixed delay (recommend five seconds). No retry on an in-progress writer. Preserve invalid partial artifacts before relaunch. On restart, consume recorded attempts for the same stable step; never reset the two-attempt budget by restarting a driver or creating a new run. Retry of a code-mutating protected implementer is outside this rule.
4. Settle the entire batch only after all original/retry writers stop. Reuse `quota.Evaluate`, membership leases, immutable kickoff/history, retained-obligation snapshots, projection receipts, consumer refresh, notices and failed-round re-evaluation. Do not make per-agent reductions as failures arrive. At least the usable implementer/designee (when designated/pinned) plus one distinct usable non-organizer must remain; before implementation, at least two usable non-organizers including any live designee. A declared organizer never counts, even if participating. For this undeclared-organizer run, codex-1 fills the single implementer slot exactly as the brief expressly authorizes; it does not supply another slot.
5. Keep facilitator, designee, pin and started consensus/FINAL/review-consensus drafter protected. Re-evaluate every reviewer, model-diversity, strict and goal-check gate. The floor allows deliberation to continue with two participants; it does not silently waive the separate auto-close requirement of two reviewers. A shortfall note recommends owner authorization of an independent reviewer process (Claude or another Codex identity, with explicit model-diversity disposition if needed), or an attended evidence-backed close/changed plan; choose none automatically.
6. Persist both failed attempts and their bound step identity as evidence in the same transition. Include invocation IDs, terminal status/exit or watchdog reason, validator reason, UTC observations, and verbatim decisive error excerpts with only secret redaction. Private full logs stay private; record truncation/redaction visibly. Every valid dissent and invalid partial file survives. A completed valid ❌ remains a valid artifact and prevents dropout at that step.
7. A participant dropped by the new trigger never rejoins that idea, even by ordinary manual/catch-up or `quota revise` paths; history, not display markers, records that terminal membership fact. A later opt-out or policy downgrade must not erase it. Next idea re-probes normally. Existing quota-only historical exclusions retain their old owner-confirmed-return semantics. A retained ❌ for a permanently dropped author consequently needs an explicit owner ruling, not a fabricated withdrawal. Reset hints are diagnostic only and carry no same-idea relaunch suggestion for dropout.

## Concerns / open questions

- The owner says keep it small, but durable retry consumption across driver restarts is the important new state. Prefer existing invocation/attempt evidence, not a general retry service. The two-attempt bound must be mechanical; organizer memory is insufficient.
- Shipped `quota.Evaluate` requires success observations for floor slots; single-signoff/draft dispatch has to include previously valid usable peers without pretending unresolved failures are successes. Verify the existing settled-batch path before changing it.
- A restarted driver cannot launch extra writers or count stale output as fresh success. Evidence needs idea + logical step + agent and immutable attempt IDs, with no proof from model prose.
- Preserve the prior release's accepted failures rather than accidentally promising them fixed. Broadening kickoff eligibility makes its missing-inbox escalation case more reachable; include a narrow diagnostic fix only if this new AC needs it, with independent review.
- The existing two-reviewer auto-close gate means a two-member survivor run can still require owner judgment at close. The brief explicitly says shortfalls escalate; do not silently change that gate in this idea.
- The product's five-second retry is a design recommendation. This idea itself follows the distinct owner instructions: provider/timeout retry waits 15 minutes and a participant is dropped after two failures. Kimi's two actual step failures are recorded separately and are not evidence that this product is already implemented.

## Risks

The central risk is mistaking system/control-plane failure for a participant failure and deleting a dissenter by manipulating a launch. Gate administrative cancellation and integrity failures, require mechanically bound two-attempt outcomes, preserve all dissent and validate the whole batch. Persistent trigger versioning prevents retroactive policy widening. Shared-volume durability and privacy of excerpts need focused probes; the prior quota reviews demonstrate why local-only unit checks are insufficient. Agreement between participants is one architecture family and is not proof that these failure paths are sound.
