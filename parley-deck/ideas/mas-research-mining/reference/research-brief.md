## 3. Cross-cutting facts about the corpus (relevant to every item below)

1. **Duplicate sources across lenses are not corroboration.** MAST (arXiv:2503.13657) was independently surfaced by **four** lenses; Refute-or-Promote, *Not All Flips*, *The Cost of Consensus*, IFScale, and arXiv:2604.02460 each by **two**. Six lenses searching overlapping literature re-find the same papers. Apparent convergence in the finding list is partly an artifact of lens overlap. (SECONDARY, established by cross-reading the six lens outputs.)
2. **Most metrics in this corpus require a ground-truth label.** Oracle gap, correct-to-wrong flips, expected correctness, task success, P(DM|D=1), Pass² — all defined against a known-correct answer. Parley's design deliberation emits a design document with no correctness label. The label-free measurements in the corpus are: modal-adoption/flip rate, acted-on fraction of findings per round, consensus level vs finding severity, run-to-run reproducibility, token/wall-clock normalization. (SECONDARY, flagged independently by two verifiers.)
3. **Almost nothing in the corpus tests Parley's workload.** Every quantitative source evaluates single-shot verifiable-answer tasks, multiple-choice benchmarks, logic puzzles, math, multi-hop QA, or ethics deliberation. **One** source (AIDev/CRA-in-PRs) measures code review at population scale, and it measures whether comments were acted on, not whether they were correct. **Zero** sources measure multi-turn software design deliberation.
4. **An unreconciled contradiction inside the corpus.** One finding advances confidence-weighted aggregation as the decisive lever (arXiv:2601.19921); another reports confidence *visibility* measured at +0.023, not significant (arXiv:2511.07784), and credits Parley for omitting it. Weighting and visibility are distinguishable; the corpus never does the distinguishing.
5. **Double-counted instruments.** "Was a concern raised in an early round still present at consensus" is proposed twice by different routes — DelibTrace fact-survival tracking (arXiv:2606.03032) and oracle gap (arXiv:2605.00914). One instrument, two findings.
6. **Five of eight findings in one lens conceded in their own analysis that Parley already has the mechanism, then argued for it anyway** (SECONDARY, debate-consensus verifier). The same pattern recurs in other lenses.
7. **Three findings that press "shrink the coordination machinery" rest on three unrelated transfer errors that happen to point the same way** (SECONDARY, failure-modes verifier): a mostly-single-agent production workload where inter-agent failure modes cannot fire by construction; a martingale theorem whose homogeneity premise Parley violates; and a paper about *automatically generated* architectures whose actual result favours hand-architected ones.

## 4. Findings that survived adversarial verification

### 4.1 Protocol size, rule count, and rule collisions

**WIRE — within-policy instruction collisions resolved silently** (arXiv:2605.27784, Yan/Chen/Zhang) — *verdict: SURVIVES*
- **MECHANISM:** Five stages over one policy document: extract prescriptive rules → encode each as a clause with activation condition, force sign, behaviour primitive, decision surface → SAT-triage every clause pair for pairs that can co-govern one decision with incompatible hard constraints → realize survivors into concrete requests ("witnesses") → run each witness and judge the response against **both** source rules independently. Stages 1–3 are static over the document; 4–5 require model trials.
- **CLAIM:** When two rules in one policy collide, the model silently picks one and emits no signal that a conflict occurred; the failure surfaces later as an agent "disobeying" a rule.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, all figures verified exactly) 6 public policies, 276 source rules, 560 clauses, 30,944 pairwise comparisons, 170 hard-collision candidates (0.55% yield), 1,402 witnesses, 13,335 jointly-governed trials. Resolution profile: 35.4% both rules satisfied / 17.7% earlier-only / 24.4% later-only / 22.5% neither — **64.6% non-joint**. Per-policy spread 23.3%–52.3%; per-model spread 10.8%–35.9%. Human validation: rule extraction 95.0%, clause encoding 93.8%, response judging 84.2%. No model acknowledged the collision. Single lab, no replication, 0.55% yield means most pipeline work is discarded.
- **PARLEY-SIDE (PRIMARY):** The Go drift guard compares two files byte-for-byte over normalized zones — it catches copy divergence, not within-copy semantic collision. `parley protocol check` (`internal/app/protocol.go:306`) is a render/hash sync check. The only conflict handling in COOPERATION.md is a set of locally-scoped override clauses. Stages 1–3 read only the document and never decide what an agent sees, so they do not inherit the deleted context-selector's proof burden.

**Instruction Stacking Collapse + IFScale — the negative result on compression** (arXiv:2608.02639, Anand & Chattaraj; arXiv:2507.11538, Jaroslawicz et al.) — *verdict: SURVIVES*
- **MECHANISM:** 24 verifier-checked atomic instructions randomly stacked at depths 1–20, per-instruction follow rate measured at each depth. Second mechanism: "prompt compilation" — a training-free single-LLM-call rewrite that clusters similar instructions, merges redundancies, and inserts explicit precedence notes.
- **CLAIM (positive half):** Compliance degrades with instruction *count* independent of task difficulty, and degrades **by silent omission** rather than by visible error. **CLAIM (negative half):** Rewriting the instruction set tighter recovers compliance only for weak models; for strong models the recovery is ~0 or negative.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, verified exactly) Depth-1 ~96% for all three models; depth-20 Claude Sonnet 4.6 60.4%, Gemini 2.5 Flash 43.3%, GPT-5-mini 20.1%. Compilation recovery +11.0pp / +3.3pp / **−1.2pp** respectively; Spearman ρ(raw follow rate, recovery) = **−0.85, p = 0.004, n = 9** across three provider ladders. IFScale independently: 20 models, 7 providers, best frontier model ~68–69% at 500 instructions; omission dominates modification at density (llama-4-scout 34.88:1); three decay shapes (threshold / linear / exponential to a 7–15% floor). **Struck by verifier:** the primacy-bias sub-argument — primacy peaks at 150–200 instructions and "uniformly diminish[es]" at 300+, converging to 1.0–1.5, so position stops mattering in the high-load regime. **Scope limits:** single-turn, single-agent, benchmark tops out at depth 20 while COOPERATION.md carries hundreds of rules — applying it to Parley is extrapolation beyond the measured range. The paper's "~12% of pairs show behavioural conflict (95% CI [4.6, 11.6])" is internally inconsistent (interval excludes its own estimate) and must not be load-bearing.
- **PARLEY-SIDE (PRIMARY):** Tracks gate which *gates apply*; every agent still reads the whole 105,382-byte file. No mechanism reduces rule count in context.

**FORGE — formal policy enforcement, and static analysis over policy text** (arXiv:2602.16708v3, Palumbo/Choudhary/Choi/Amir/Chalasani/Jha) — *verdict: SURVIVES on the static-analysis half*
- **MECHANISM:** Policies expressed in Datalog with stratified negation; an aspect weaver intercepts candidate actions at join points; a reference monitor returns allow/deny before execution. **Separately**, four static analyses run over the policy text alone, independent of any execution: contradiction ("both Allow(v) and Deny(v,m) derivable in the same substrate state"), redundancy ("removing it leaves the verdict unchanged on every action"), subsumption, and conditional reachability.
- **CLAIM:** The gap between "the policy says so" and "the policy is enforced" is large and closable by moving checkable rules out of prose.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, quotes verified) τ2-Bench 58% → 98% across three frontier models; prompt injection 0/5 vs 5/5; MALADE 0/5 → 5/5 (15 trials); latency ~28% / 19–38%; <$0.05 per trial. Theorem 1 (verdict correctness) verified. Two of three studies have very small n; no independent replication. **Verifier scope corrections the researcher did not make:** the 58→98 result comes from intercepting **tool invocations** at join points, and most of COOPERATION.md governs prose and process with no interceptable action ("address every other active agent explicitly" has no join point); and translating Parley's prose rules into Datalog is the hard step, costed by neither the paper nor the finding.
- **PARLEY-SIDE (PRIMARY):** No machine-readable representation of the protocol's own rules exists. Contradiction/redundancy/subsumption analysis over the protocol is absent. Parley already makes this move at small scale — roster authority in `agents.toml` with a generated, explicitly non-authoritative §2 view (line 1094).

**Claude's Constitution — the largest deployed agent constitution is deliberately not kept small** (anthropic.com/constitution, Jan 2026; ablation: alignment.anthropic.com/2026/teaching-claude-why/) — *verdict: SURVIVES on mechanism (a)*
- **MECHANISM:** Three structural choices in a large, deliberately non-minimal document: (a) **one document-wide priority order** resolving all conflicts; (b) a separate **lower tier** holding situation-specific instructions so new special cases do not add rules to the core; (c) state reasons rather than enumerate rules. Maintained as a versioned living document at a canonical URL.
- **CLAIM:** Constitutions are not kept small by shrinking them; conflicts are handled by a global precedence order rather than by deduplication.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY) Priority statement verified verbatim: "In cases of apparent conflict, Claude should generally prioritize these properties in the order in which they are listed: (1) Broadly safe, (2) Broadly ethical, (3) Compliant with Anthropic's guidelines, (4) Genuinely helpful." Guidelines tier verified as a distinct lower tier. **Evidence tier: first-party design rationale with zero empirical support** — the announcement cites no studies. "Should *generally* prioritize" is a default with judgment, not strict lexical precedence. **Omission that inverts the reasons-over-rules half:** the finding cites 3% misalignment (reasons, 3M tokens) vs 15% (honeypot, 30M tokens) but omits that the **scaled 85M-token rule-demonstration dataset reached ~1%** — better than reasons on the headline metric. Also, this measures **training-data composition**, not a document read at inference time.
- **PARLEY-SIDE (PRIMARY):** No document-wide order across the 18 sections. §15 has already had to declare pairwise subordination twice (lines 1235, 1322), alongside local overrides elsewhere. The tier mechanism already exists in Parley for playbooks (line 1187) but has never been applied to the protocol's own sections.

### 4.2 The review / fix-up loop: stopping, admission, and whether a finding was earned by a run

**Detection-Without-Correction — two-parameter decomposition of multi-stage pipelines** (arXiv:2605.27559v1, Nilayam/Ramanna/Tumbade, ServiceNow) — *verdict: SURVIVES*
- **MECHANISM:** Decompose each downstream response into detection D (treat upstream as non-authoritative and revise) and conditional generation G (is the revision correct). Crossed with upstream ground truth gives four regimes: BOUNDARY, IP (incorrect propagated), DC (detect-correct), DM (detect-**mis**correct). A deterministic rubric classifies each round-to-round transition, yielding two parameters: detection rate P(D=1) and **conditional miscorrection rate P(DM|D=1)**.
- **CLAIM:** The load-bearing quantity in a verify-repair pipeline is P(DM|D=1), not objection frequency — and adding rounds cannot move it.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, every number verified) 5 models × 4 families × 4 benchmarks × 2 methods = 14 cohorts. P(DM|D=1) range **53.45–93.66%**; point estimate >0.5 in 14/14 cohorts; Wilson-95% lower bound >0.5 in 13/14. Detection rate 1.26–20.97%. Rubric-vs-annotator agreement 73.3% raw → 93.3%. **Limits stronger than the finding stated:** agents homogeneous within cohort, ground truth answer-extractive and mostly binary, and the authors explicitly say the dominance claim "may require recalibration where ground truth is less binary" and that generalization to "non-extractive tasks (open-ended generation, code synthesis with multiple correct solutions)" is an open empirical question — **which is precisely Parley's regime**.
- **PARLEY-SIDE (PRIMARY):** Nothing in the Go source separates "the reviewer was right to object" from "the fix was correct". A fix that gets re-BLOCKed on the same claim is an observed DM and is currently never counted.

**Refute-or-Promote — adversarial stage gates with a mandatory empirical kill test** (arXiv:2604.19049, Agarwal) — *verdict: SURVIVES on the empirical-gate residual; OVERSTATED on the adversarial-framing claim*
- **MECHANISM:** Candidates must **survive** sequential stage gates rather than accumulate agreement. At each gate the reviewer is given an adversarial **kill mandate** (objective: disprove, not assess). Cold-start reviewers spawned without prior discussion context. A cross-model critic from a different family. **A mandatory executed test before promotion.**
- **CLAIM (as written):** Flipping the reviewer's objective from evaluate to kill restores precision. **CLAIM (as it survives):** The demonstrated filter is **execution**, not framing.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY) 31 days, 7 targets, ~171 candidates, ~79% killed retrospectively, 83% on a prospective subset (n=30). External outcomes real: 4 CVEs, LWG 4549, 5 merged C++ PRs, 3 compiler conformance bugs, 8 fixes without CVE. **The paper disclaims every causal inference:** "No ablation studies isolating individual mechanisms"; no control arm; "Protocol evolution as confounder"; target selection, operator learning, and manual prompt iteration named as confounders; "No vulnerability was discovered autonomously." **The flagship anecdote inverts the framing thesis:** ten agents including a senior-tier arbiter, under the adversarial mandate, unanimously confirmed a non-existent Bleichenbacher padding oracle — "One test killed what 80+ agents' reasoning could not."
- **PARLEY-SIDE (PRIMARY):** §15.4 (line 1321) explicitly carves findings *out* of the provenance gate: "It does not gate what a reviewer may report." LE-1 requires the reviewer to "attempt a failing case **or** run the relevant check" — a disjunction governing reviewer diligence, not a finding's admissibility. **A CRITICAL asserted from RECALL can open a full fix-up cycle today.** Verifier note: this is the only mechanism in the entire survey aimed at review **round count** rather than review quality. **REFUTED sub-claim:** the finding's assertion that Parley "structurally forbids" cold-start reviewers is false (line 324). The true narrower fact: independence is enforced at design round 1 and **not** at Phase 6 — the two phases whose measured round counts diverged (1.6 flat vs 5.1 exploding). The **context-asymmetry** component collides head-on with the standard that killed the context selector.

**FactReview — execution-based claim verification** (arXiv:2604.04074, Yue et al.) — *verdict: OVERSTATED headline, one component survives*
- **MECHANISM:** Four stages: claim extraction with type/scope/source location; literature positioning; **reference verification** (normalize title/author/year/venue/ID/DOI, resolve against arXiv, Semantic Scholar, OpenReview, OpenAlex, flag unresolved/withdrawn/mismatched with repair suggestions); and **execution-based verification** that actually runs the artifact's repo. Issues no accept/reject — audit layer only.
- **CLAIM:** Execution is the single largest evidence source in claim verification.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, ablations verified exactly) Removing execution flips **17.0%** of claim statuses — the largest single ablation; literature 5.7%, semantic retrieval 8.4%, manuscript-only 26.1%. 84.4% of 463 major claims across 35 papers; review time 50.6 → 21.3 min; coverage 86.9% → 98.9%. The headline quality table (4.86 vs 4.17 vs 3.63 vs 3.33) rests on a self-designed "evidence-focused rubric" whose scorer the paper does not name; the authors concede the comparison "reflects our evidence-aware rubric rather than venue-specific reviewing criteria".
- **REFUTED headline:** "Parley tags where a claim came from but not whether the evidence supports it" is false. Line 1245 defines CONFIRMED/WRONG/UNVERIFIED and the §15.2 table's **Maximum verdict** column makes provenance *cap* the support verdict; §15.3 adds DISPUTED. That is already a two-axis system, and Parley additionally has WRONG, which FactReview lacks. **Only "Partially supported" is genuinely missing.**
- **What survives:** stage (3) generalized — a mechanical resolver for **internal** cross-references. Parley has exactly one hand-built single-purpose instance (the two-file drift guard), and it is a measured failure (missed the third copy).

**VRR-Stop — noisy verify-repair loops destroy correct work** (arXiv:2607.17641, Wu et al.) — *verdict: OVERSTATED because its Parley model is wrong; the source result stands*
- **MECHANISM:** Four-parameter noise model — ρ₀ false acceptance, ρ₁ false rejection, α repair benefit, β repair damage — with verifier discrimination J = 1 − ρ₀ − ρ₁. Maintains a Bayesian posterior that the artifact is valid; stops when expected marginal gain goes non-positive at belief* = α/(α+β). Fallback VRR-Guard for J ≤ 0.15 accepts a new candidate only if it beats the incumbent by a retention margin.
- **CLAIM:** With a noisy verifier, **acceptance rises while true validity falls**, so a converging acceptance signal is not evidence of convergence.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, verified exactly) Stress setting: VRR-Stop 0.722 true validity at 0.72 mean repair rounds vs fixed 5-round repair **0.116** (60.6pp gap); no-repair baseline 0.700. True validity peaks then collapses in 6 of 8 settings; damage probabilities β from 0.615 to 0.938; validity 0.45 → 0.87 by round 2 → 0.12 by round 6. On N=500 stress traces, "**55% of instances see a correct plan repaired into an incorrect one, and 24% of these damaging repairs win majority acceptance**". Benchmarks are reasoning/code/tool-use, not design deliberation; no replication.
- **CORRECTION (PRIMARY, load-bearing):** The finding claims Parley's caps "are precisely the fixed K baseline this paper beats by 60.6pp". **False** — lines 646–666 state Parley judges by trajectory, not a pass counter, and that budgets "are escalation thresholds, not close criteria". The accurate framing is different and sharper: the paper **refutes the heuristic Parley actually runs** — line 657–659 defines converging as "total findings dropping sharply each pass", which is exactly the acceptance signal the paper proves can rise while true validity falls. Residual novelty (no belief state, no false-accept/reject estimate, no damage term β, no retention margin) is real but smaller than stated.

**Iterative self-repair — two repair rounds capture 76–95%, when the verifier is deterministic** (arXiv:2604.10508v1, Arimbur) — *verdict: OVERSTATED as standalone; functions as a control case*
- **MECHANISM:** R0 generation plus up to 4 repair rounds against a **deterministic** verifier (benchmark unit tests, sandboxed, 15s per case); marginal pass@1 gain per round; 7 models, HumanEval (164) and MBPP Sanitized (257).
- **CLAIM:** Marginal returns collapse after round 2.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, verified) HumanEval R1 gains +2.4pp (Qwen3 32B) to +9.8pp (Gemini 2.5 Pro); R3/R4 collapse toward zero. "Two repair rounds capture the majority (76–95%)" is a verbatim Discussion quote whose calculation is **not shown in the main results**. Error thrashing documented qualitatively (Maverick cycling assertion → name → assertion → timeout). The finding's "no solution degradation observed" could not be confirmed and reads as inference from silence.
- **STATUS:** Its value is as the **J≈1 control** isolating the variable in VRR-Stop: with a deterministic verifier extra rounds are merely wasteful; with a noisy verifier they are destructive. Its one novel proposal — split the fix-up budget by verifier type — is tested by **neither** paper. Parley already has a verifier-type asymmetry (line 592, LE-4).

**Code review agents in real pull requests** (arXiv:2604.03196) — *verdict: SURVIVES for the instrumentation gap only*
- **MECHANISM:** Observational mining of the AIDev dataset: 3,109 unique PRs in commented-review state out of 19,450, contrasting human-only against agent-only review across 13 distinct CRAs, with an author-constructed "signal ratio" (was the comment acted on).
- **CLAIM (as written):** Agent-only review correlates with worse outcomes. **CLAIM (as it survives):** General-purpose agent review produces mostly non-actionable commentary.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, verbatim confirmed) "CRA-only PRs achieve a 45.20% merge rate, 23.17 percentage points lower than human-only PRs (68.37%)"; "60.2% of closed CRA-only PRs fall into the 0–30% signal range, and 12 of 13 CRAs exhibit average signal ratios below 60%". **Explicitly correlational** — PRs that attract only bot review plausibly differ in triviality, ownership, project health; the abandonment analysis rests on 98 closed PRs. Signal ratio measures whether a comment was *acted on*, not whether it was *correct*, so it says nothing about rubber-stamping or false negatives. **Transfer defect the finding under-prices:** open-source PRs impose no obligation to respond, whereas Parley requires every finding to be dispositioned — so the population generating the low signal is structurally unlike Parley's.
- **PARLEY-SIDE:** Parley records dispositions but never computes what fraction of findings per round were acted on, leaving §6.5's churn heuristic to agent judgement when it could be computed from data already on disk.

### 4.3 Conformity, silence, and consent

**Not All Flips Are Conformity — decomposing stance convergence** (arXiv:2606.00820, Hao/Wu/Qiu/Xiao/Xu/Zheng/Qin) — *verdict: SURVIVES; surfaced independently by two lenses; both verifiers failed to break it*
- **MECHANISM:** Counterfactual isolation of three mechanisms the standard flip-rate metric conflates: a self-reflection-only baseline (spontaneous instability), an "information gradient" condition supplying **deliberately vacuous peer reasoning** (bare-stance conformity), and the residual (genuine persuasion). Plus a pre-hoc predictor of harmful flips, plus a targeted intervention.
- **CLAIM:** A large share of position change in debate is noise or bare conformity rather than persuasion, and conformity flips are predominantly correct→wrong; **argument-shaped but contentless text still moves peers who resisted the bare position.**
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, every figure verified twice) 37% spontaneous instability under self-reflection alone; strict conformity 29.30% [27.8, 30.9] MMLU-Pro and 29.15% [24.4, 34.4] GPQA-Diamond; **63.6% correct-to-wrong** primary, 57–77% across model families; vacuous reasoning drives 20–39% error adoption (CI [22.8, 37.5] around a 29.66% point estimate); harmful-flip predictor ROC-AUC 0.79; intervention −13.6pp, p<0.001. GPT-4o primary, robustness on GPQA-Diamond/BBEH/AGIEval, cross-model on DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Qwen-Plus. Preprint, no replication, multiple-choice only. **An attempted refutation backfired and is reported honestly:** initial accuracy on disagreement questions is 36.9%, so direction-neutral flipping predicts ~36.9% correct-to-wrong; the observed 63.6% far exceeds it, meaning the harm asymmetry is **better supported than the authors themselves argue**. The paper's own negative result: reducing peer influence *without* correctness labels fails, because beneficial and detrimental influence are not separable from outside.
- **PARLEY-SIDE (PRIMARY + verifier):** §5 taxes blocking (a block requires a counter-proposal) but imposes **no symmetric cost on withdrawing** a block. §15.6 correlated-agreement fires only when round 1 closes with no substantive disagreement, so it cannot catch dissent-in-round-1-then-fold-in-round-2 — exactly the measured conformity path. §15.5 requires drafters to record position changes, which is bookkeeping, not justification. Line 350's "silence = implicit agreement" scores an agent that went quiet because everyone agreed identically to one that was convinced. **Label-free**, so it works on design deliberation.

**CosMAD / ColMAD — consensus-seeking debate suppresses correct objections** (arXiv:2510.20963v2, Chen/Niu/Cheng/Han/Sugiyama) — *verdict: OVERSTATED; one half falsified, one half clean*
- **MECHANISM:** Separates debate into competitive (CopMAD) and consensus-seeking (CosMAD) regimes; proposes ColMAD framing debate as non-zero-sum, requiring agents to quote evidence **with exact-match verification against the source**, to self-audit their own claims, and scoring them on complementing rather than defeating each other.
- **CLAIM:** A strong agent voluntarily abandons its own correct judgment to converge; and executed quote-checking is what recovers the loss.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, verified) CopMAD collapses to 24.89 avg F2 vs ~74–84 single-agent baselines; CosMAD 71–76 F2, **below simple Voting**; cross-family oracle pairs could eliminate **>30%** of errors; ColMAD 86.29 F2 vs 82.20 best individual (+4pp), up to +10pp over CopMAD/CosMAD; token-matched at ~14.7K, 86.29 vs self-consistency 81.98; Anthropic-Harmful attack success 14.59–31.69% → near 0%. Single lab, no replication.
- **FALSIFIED HALF (PRIMARY):** "Silence and reasoned concession are indistinguishable today" is false for the review phase — lines 552–556 require a per-disposition concurrence decision and a recorded close path; line 690 provides a purpose-built escalation for exactly this failure (weakened only by being voluntary and self-triggered). The claim holds only for **design rounds** under line 350.
- **CLEAN HALF (PRIMARY):** §15.2 requires PRIMARY to quote "a stable locator and the relevant passage", but **nothing executes the match** — the tag is self-declared. ColMAD's contribution is that the match is executed. Note also: the **>30% cross-family oracle result points in favour of** a heterogeneous roster.

**The Deliberative Deficit — procedurally excellent, epistemically shallow** (arXiv:2608.10186v1, Flechtner) — *verdict: OVERSTATED on heterogeneity; the procedural-vs-outcome result stands*
- **MECHANISM:** 1,980 five-agent deliberation runs over 12 citizen-assembly topics, 11 frontier configurations, 3 prompting treatments × 5 replicates, two rounds, randomized speaking order. Measures **three things separately**: procedural quality (Discourse Quality Index via automated AQuA), outcome quality (Deliberative Reason Index — intersubjective consistency between how a participant weights considerations and their stated preferences), and perspective diversity. Benchmarked against a human citizen assembly (N=407) and Europolis (N=910).
- **CLAIM:** Procedural parity with humans, ~30% of the human outcome gain — process metrics certify very little about outcome quality.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, verified exactly) AQuA 2.939 vs 2.980 (Holm-corrected p=0.052); ΔDRI 0.029 vs 0.099; initial diversity 6.51 vs 18.78; humans converge −1.21 while LLMs diverge +0.26/+0.37; persona ablation induced 27.7 diversity with ΔDRI **−0.058, p=0.456**. Domain is value-laden political questions with no ground truth.
- **STRUCK by verifier:** the claim that this "directly tests Parley's heterogeneous-roster premise". The five-agent groups were **homogeneous by default** (same model); mixed-family ensembles were a separate variant showing **higher** starting diversity (~9.5 vs 6.51) and were **not** the condition carried into the DRI ablation; the ablation induced diversity by **persona prompting within single models**, in a pilot of three topics across two models (N=60). The paper's own model families closely mirror Parley's roster, and the directly relevant condition was left as a footnote.
- **PARLEY-SIDE:** Every health signal Parley exposes is procedural — all-check signoffs, complete round files, DISPUTED handled, round counts, track compliance. This is a measured wedge between such signals and outcome quality.

**The Deliberative Illusion / DelibTrace** (arXiv:2606.03032, Wan et al.) — *verdict: OVERSTATED — it is a confirmation, with one instrument residue*
- **MECHANISM:** Atomic-fact decomposition, criticality labelling, facts distributed across agents, **per-round survival tracking**, Shannon stance entropy, three topologies.
- **CLAIM:** Facts attrit and stances homogenize across deliberation rounds.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY) GPT-4.1 fully-connected: system-level critical-fact retention 79.0 / 60.1 / **46.5** across rounds; agent level 35.0 / 24.7 / 18.3. Adversarial ablation 58.9 / 37.4 / 19.3 vs 46.5 / 82.4. Tree topology round-3 35.3% — worse than fully-connected. Domain is **ethics** (Scruples AITA, 710 instances) and **news** (1,044), with no verifiable ground truth. Persona prompting and model heterogeneity were tested as mitigations with limited benefit. **Two researcher errors, both favouring the finding:** "72.4% critical-fact loss" is the abstract's cross-condition worst case, not the cited cell (0.47 retention = 53% loss); and "the paper explicitly proposes no mitigation" is false — it calls for "preservation-oriented protocols" including **shared evidence ledgers and fact-level memory**, which is a description of Parley's append-only artifact store.
- **RESIDUE:** DelibTrace is a published, concrete instrument for the exact claim that killed the context-selection mechanism — seed known objections, track survival to the closing round. Parley has no such instrument. The transplant cost is the hard part: defining an "atomic objection" in a code review is unsolved and this paper does not help.

### 4.4 Context: quadratic growth, attention, and auditable non-drop

**ARC — Addressable Recall Compaction** (arXiv:2607.25066) — *verdict: SURVIVES; the context-memory verifier called it the strongest item in that lens*
- **MECHANISM:** Deterministic, **LLM-free** compaction of tool observations into bounded, addressable stubs, with an explicit recall action to re-materialize any archived item exactly, and a bounded active view. Nothing is discarded.
- **CLAIM:** Guarantees prompt safety, stable archival fidelity, persistent finite discoverability, exact address-conditioned recall without re-execution, observational losslessness, and separation of active from archival growth (Theorem 15). Proposition 9: if each visible citation costs ≥ c_min tokens and all n must be simultaneously visible, no constant bounds the active prompt — **pagination is forced**.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, all four cells exact) NIH Qwen3-8B ARC 99.00 vs RAG-memory 79.57; Qwen3-32B 99.80 vs 96.67. LongBench-v2 Hard 8B 27.47 vs LLM_summary 25.83; 32B 32.47 vs sliding_window 30.67. **Decisive caveat quoted near-verbatim from Remark 16:** "It does not prove that the language model will select the correct identifier, issue enough recall commands, or solve the downstream task correctly… a mechanism-level guarantee." Qwen3-only, single preprint, thin LongBench margins the authors themselves downplay. Targets tool observations inside one agent's loop, not multi-agent artifacts — the transfer is an analogy.
- **PARLEY-SIDE:** Parley has the append-only content-addressable store; it lacks bounded stubs, a recall action, and a bounded active view. **Discount:** Parley agents can already read any round file on demand, so "recall" informally exists. What is genuinely missing is the **audit log of what was and was not opened**. The verifier's note: the reframing changes the *type* of the unverifiable step, from "the summarizer preserved every objection" (an uncheckable property of generated prose) to "agent X did not issue `_recall` for §ab12c3" (a logged, mechanically auditable fact). The proposed signoff gate built on this is the researcher's invention, not the paper's.

**Context Rot** (Chroma technical report, July 2025; corroborated by arXiv:2602.07962 LOCA-bench) — *verdict: SURVIVES*
- **MECHANISM:** Input length varied with task held fixed, across NIAH variants, LongMemEval, and a trivial repeated-words replication task, plus needle-question similarity, distractors, and haystack coherence. 18 models including Claude 4, GPT-4.1, Gemini 2.5, Qwen3.
- **CLAIM:** Degradation with length is not a reasoning-difficulty effect; lower needle-question similarity degrades faster; a **single distractor** lowers accuracy and distractor types differ in harm; and — counter-intuitively — models perform **worse when the haystack preserves a logical flow of ideas**, with shuffling consistently improving performance.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, all key results confirmed) Strong descriptive, weak causal. **Not peer-reviewed**, and the verifier notes the researcher correctly disclosed Chroma's commercial interest in the conclusion that long context is unreliable. **Discount:** Chroma's "coherent haystack" was continuous prose; Parley round files are structurally delimited, headed, and per-agent attributed, so "close to a worst case for attention" is an untested analogy.
- **PARLEY-SIDE (PRIMARY):** No `salien`, no `superseded`, no open-vs-resolved distinction at re-send time. §15's DISPUTED is currently a verdict-resolution device only. **The conceptual move that survives:** Parley's completeness guarantee is a guarantee about the **prompt**, not about the **read**. Under "silence = agreement", an objection that is present-but-unattended produces the same recorded-consent-never-given outcome as one that was dropped — a hole in the safety argument used to justify deleting the context selector. The distractor asymmetry is the consequence: pruning **resolved** material is a categorically different proposition from pruning **open objections**.

**Omission taxonomy + canary checkpoint taps** (arXiv:2607.22448, Rajan) — *verdict: OVERSTATED, with one crux that holds*
- **MECHANISM:** Nine-layer taxonomy L0–L8 splitting **deterministic** layers (ingestion/redaction, tool protocol, orchestrator middleware incl. history condensation, tokenization) from **behavioral** (engine, attention/positional, priors, decoding, agent loop/compaction). Attribution by **canary**: unique strings in needle facts, checkpoint taps T0–T7 doing exact-match presence verification at every boundary, a failure ledger, and an omission waterfall decomposing loss per layer.
- **CLAIM (crux, survives as directional):** "Send everything to everyone" is **not a neutral safe default** — long context is itself an omission mechanism, relocating loss from an auditable middleware layer into attention, where it is silent and unattributable.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY) Per-layer ω verified exactly: L0 0.160, L1 0.175, L2 0.165, L3 0.000, L4 0.000, **L5 attention 0.189 (largest behavioral layer)**, L6 0.012, L7 0.018, L8 0.065. The load-bearing contrast **OR 7.43 [5.44, 10.15] for 32k vs 2k** is a behavioral factorial contrast belonging to L5, verified as such. 75,476 **synthetic, fault-injected** trials plus a 372-trial real-data pilot (FHIR 0.148 vs PubMed 0.889 — large source variance). Behavioral labels rest on a heuristic −2.0 forced-logprob threshold. **MUST NOT be cited as a base rate:** the paper states "This is a controlled benchmark decomposition, not an estimate of omission prevalence in production" and that the 73.4% deterministic share "describes this benchmark allocation" because Phase A **deliberately injects** redaction, pagination and truncation failures. **Correction against the finding's own interest:** canary taps T0–T7 are **implemented**, not future scope; what is future work is a *detector*, and no no-drop guarantee is formalized anywhere. Needle-retrieval in a 32k haystack is an analogy for, not a measurement of, an agent missing a participant objection in a 105,382-byte protocol.

**TRACE — execution instability under compression** (arXiv:2608.06503) — *verdict: OVERSTATED; one metric transfers cleanly*
- **MECHANISM:** Measures execution stability under context compression, using **Pass²** (the paper's P^k: the fraction of tasks solved in **all** k independent runs) alongside Pass@k.
- **CLAIM (as written):** FIFO truncation beats summary-based compression. **CLAIM (as verified):** FIFO **matches or slightly exceeds** summary-based at 16K/8K/4K; the split (72.8% vs 42.2%) emerges only at a budget **tighter than 4K**.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY) Behavioural numbers verified exactly: divergence 0.149 raw → 0.233 compressed → 0.289 omitted; POST produces 0.108 more blocked/error actions than PRE at the first action; TRACE 77.1 vs Prompting-O 71.4; Pass² 67.3 vs 59.5; Kimi transfer 84.5 / 79.2. **Two citation errors:** the "4K" label is wrong, and "at 4K, Pass² falls to 52.4% (from 77.4%) while Pass@2 stays at 74.6%" is **stitched from three unrelated Table 1 cells** (52.4/74.6 are TRACE's Hard-subset method row; 77.4 is No-compression *average* Pass²; Hard-subset uncompressed Pass² is 65.1). The underlying claim is nonetheless true in the real table: FIFO Average 63.7/53.0/74.4 vs uncompressed 85.7/77.4/94.0; FIFO Hard Pass² 19.0 vs 65.1. **Material scope omission:** the paper explicitly scopes the FIFO result to an "information-refetchable setting" and warns the finding differs "where summary-based compaction is expected to outperform FIFO truncation" — a condition that probably **holds** for Parley, whose round files stay on disk.
- **PARLEY-SIDE (PRIMARY):** No Pass²-equivalent exists; §15's DISPUTED handles disagreement *between agents within one run*, not variance *across runs of the same protocol*. Pass² transfers regardless of the citation errors.

**Anthropic orchestrator-worker: subagents write artifacts directly** (anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system; claude.com/blog/building-multi-agent-systems-when-and-how-to-use-them) — *verdict: SURVIVES, but not on the half the finding leads with*
- **MECHANISM:** Subagents write outputs to a shared store and hand the lead a lightweight **reference**, so full outputs never transit the orchestrator's context; decomposition by **context boundary** rather than by problem type; **verification subagents** as the one endorsed coding pattern.
- **CLAIM:** "Verification requires minimal context transfer by nature, so a verifier can blackbox-test a system without needing the full history of how it was built."
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, all quotes verbatim) **Weakest evidence tier of any surviving item.** The 90.2% figure is a vendor self-report on a private eval with no published methodology, and it measures the orchestrator-worker system as a whole — **the artifact-reference primitive has no measurement behind it at all**. Token multiples (4× / 15× / 3–10×) are unpublished production figures. The negative statements are credible precisely because they cut against vendor interest: "Most coding tasks involve fewer truly parallelizable tasks than research"; "some domains that require all agents to share the same context or involve many dependencies between agents are not a good fit for multi-agent systems today"; and a months-of-multi-agent-architecture-versus-better-prompting admission.
- **COLLISION (explicit):** Handing a pointer instead of text is still a decision about what an agent sees; an agent that does not follow the pointer produces recorded consent under "silence = agreement". The finding names this and does not resolve it.
- **CONVERGENCE (SECONDARY, orchestration verifier's flag):** four independent lines point at the same lever — **give reviewers less context, not designers**: Anthropic's verification claim, Refute-or-Promote's cold-start reviewers, the ICML "teams hold experts back" result, and Parley's own 1.6-vs-5.1 split between the phase that enforces independence and the phase that does not. No one of these is strong; the agreement across four unrelated lines is what the verifier flagged.

**Claude Code cross-session messaging** (code.claude.com/docs/en/cross-session-messaging) — *verdict: OVERSTATED; the load-bearing claim is refuted by its own source*
- **MECHANISM:** Three typed inbound outcomes (Delivered/Held/Refused) governed by a permission-mode default; partial sender-side receipts; published finite queue bounds with oldest-drop overflow (100 held, 50 accepted-unread per session); five-minute dialog expiry; and the structural rule that "a message from another session never counts as your consent".
- **CLAIM:** Every non-delivery is a first-class, typed, sender-visible event.
- **REFUTED (SECONDARY):** The sentence immediately following the quoted passage reads "**A message refused on arrival produces no sender-side notice.**" The 100-message bound "drops the oldest" with no per-message receipt. Two of the three non-delivery paths are silent by the source's own text. Anthropic explicitly refuses to claim reliable delivery. One quote was misattributed to this page from the agent-teams page and describes **mailbox files**, whereas cross-session messaging uses a per-session Unix socket. **Structural mismatch:** held/refused is the *receiver's* operator-controlled permission boundary and no consent is ever inferred from non-delivery; Parley's hazard is the opposite.
- **What remains:** the reframe — *auditable non-delivery* instead of *guaranteed delivery* — as a design angle on the deleted context selector, not as evidence that the pattern works. Parley already has a positive per-round acknowledgment receipt most frameworks lack (`driver.go:363-372` requires `responding-to` frontmatter plus a per-agent `### @<other>` heading from round 2 on); the §8 inbox has no receipt, and line 802 makes that low-stakes by design.

### 4.5 Does the multi-agent structure pay at all

**Compute-normalized evaluation** (arXiv:2502.08788, Zhang et al.; arXiv:2604.02460v1, Tran & Kiela, Stanford) — *verdict: SURVIVES on the measurement-discipline half; surfaced by two lenses*
- **MECHANISM:** (1) Sweeps round count **and** agent count against CoT and Self-Consistency at matched token spend across 5 frameworks × 9 benchmarks × 4 models, then adds Heter-MAD (agents drawn from different model families per step). (2) Caps "thinking tokens" identically across single-agent, sequential, subtask-parallel, parallel-roles, debate and ensemble at 6 budget levels, framed by the Data Processing Inequality — "the multi-agent architecture cannot increase mutual information with the true answer".
- **CLAIM:** Most measured MAD gains disappear under compute normalization; model **heterogeneity** is what survives.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, verified verbatim) Source (1): adding agents or rounds did not improve accuracy, and "performance often stagnated or declined with more computational resources, indicating that suboptimal configurations were not the primary cause" — **a sweep, not a single increment**. Heter-SoM achieved the highest overall MAD performance, "even surpassing the average Chain-of-Thought performance of individual models by up to 5.8%". Source (2): 95% bootstrap CIs frequently overlap, so the defensible claim is "gains largely disappear under normalization"; the authors disclose "significant artifacts in API-based budget control (particularly in Gemini 2.5) and in standard benchmarks, both of which can inflate apparent gains from MAS"; and they state the escape clause explicitly — "multi-agent systems become competitive when a single agent's effective context utilization is degraded, or when more compute is expended." A counterexample where debate **beat** single-agent is volunteered (1k tokens, MuSiQue, Gemini 2.5 Pro: 0.444 vs 0.413). Both preprints; neither tests software design or code review. Three model families in (2): Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama, Gemini 2.5.
- **Counter-argument supplied by the researcher, not the paper:** DPI argues against splitting **one model's** reasoning across boundaries and says nothing against combining agents with **different priors and different training data**.
- **PARLEY-SIDE (PRIMARY):** Nothing in §13 retro or the run event log normalizes outcome quality by token or wall-clock spend, and `grep single-agent` over COOPERATION.md returns nothing. §13 compares Parley runs to other Parley runs. With +112% protocol growth, 3.3× median wall clock and confirmed quadratic context growth, **every historical Parley claim about whether more rounds helped is compute-confounded** — including any conclusion drawn from the 1.6→5.1 series itself.

**MAST — multi-agent system failure taxonomy** (arXiv:2503.13657, Cemri et al., NeurIPS 2025) — *verdict: OVERSTATED as a mapping; the intervention-ceiling result survives; **surfaced by four separate lenses***
- **MECHANISM:** 150 traces hand-annotated by experts iterated to κ=0.88, then 1,600+ traces across 7 MAS frameworks labelled, yielding **14 failure modes in 3 categories** with a measured distribution, plus an LLM-judge annotator. Two intervention case studies on ChatDev.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, all 14 percentages verified to one decimal by two independent verifiers) disobey task spec 11.8, disobey role spec 1.5, **step repetition 15.7**, loss of conversation history 2.8, **unaware of termination conditions 12.4**, conversation reset 2.2, fail to ask clarification 6.8, task derailment 7.4, information withholding 0.85, ignored other agent's input 1.9, reasoning-action mismatch 13.2, premature termination 6.2, no/incomplete verification 8.2, incorrect verification 9.1. Interventions: **+9.4%** (role specification) and **+15.6%** (task-objective verification), both on ChatDev/ProgramDev. Peer-reviewed with released dataset — the strongest evidence tier in the corpus. Object studied: early-2025 open-source auto-orchestrated frameworks on 2024-era models.
- **REFUTED mapping:** FM-1.3 "step repetition" is defined as "unnecessary reiteration of previously completed steps in a process" — an agent redundantly redoing work inside one trace. Parley's review rounds find **new** defects and trigger fixes. The single largest claimed mapping does not hold. Venue was cited two different ways across lenses (Spotlight poster vs Datasets & Benchmarks spotlight); resolved as NeurIPS 2025, track label immaterial.
- **What survives (stated by three of four lenses that found it):** the **intervention ceiling**. The highest-yield intervention measured anywhere in this corpus is +15.6%, and the authors say explicitly that is not enough — "not all failure modes are resolved, and task completion rates still remain low, indicating that more substantial improvements are needed" and that reliability "may require combinatorial changes ranging from agent system organization to model level improvements." One verifier notes MAST's most effective intervention was **adding verification**, which argues for keeping §15 rather than trimming it for size.
- **A sampling caution on the distribution:** context-loss modes total 5.55%, but MAST's corpus is built from mostly short-horizon frameworks that largely do not compact, so a low context-loss share there is partly a property of the corpus. Parley's own loss-of-history rate is 0% **because** it never drops anything.

**The Illusion of Multi-Agent Advantage** (arXiv:2606.13003) — *verdict: OVERSTATED; near zero evidential value for Parley*
- **MECHANISM:** Auto-generated MAS vs CoT-SC on reasoning datasets and BrowseComp-Plus, plus a synthetic diagnostic dataset **engineered to favour MAS**, plus auto-generated vs expert-hand-architected comparison.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, verbatim) "automatic MAS consistently underperform CoT-SC despite being up to 10× more expensive"; "architectural bloat that prioritizes superficial complexity which does not translate into functional utility"; and — decisively — "**expert-architected MAS consistently outperforms automatically generated architectures**." The object is *automated architecture search*; nothing in the paper measures protocol text length, rule count, or compliance. The borrowed phrase describes the output of automated search, not a hand-written protocol. The 10× is cost, not a performance delta. **The paper's actual result points in Parley's favour.**

### 4.6 Aggregation, judging, weighting — the sub-corpus that asks Parley to reverse ratified decisions

These four are grouped because they share a structural property: each proposes a mechanism Parley **explicitly ruled out**, not one it overlooked. All four were graded OVERSTATED.

**Demystifying Multi-Agent Debate — confidence-weighted aggregation** (arXiv:2601.19921, Zhu et al.)
- **MECHANISM:** Dirichlet-Categorical belief model; diversity-aware initialization (oversample then select a diverse subset) plus confidence-weighted peer aggregation. Eq. 14 shows vanilla debate is a martingale (E[p_{i,t}|F_{t-1}] = p_{i,t-1}); Theorem 1 makes it a **sub**martingale under confidence weighting.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY) Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct only — **two 7–8B open models**, six benchmarks. **Omitted control that decides the cost question:** the same table reports Single Model = 0.908, identical to Majority Vote; the full stacked intervention reaches 0.932, i.e. **+2.4pp over one model call**, while the debate component alone (0.847) is **6.1pp worse** than a single model. No accuracy-vs-round curve exists; round dynamics appear only as round-1-to-5 Sankey plots. Diversity correlations r=0.070 (p<0.001) and r=0.019 (p=0.002) are significant only because n=26,624. The theorem's assumptions are homogeneous agents, shared priors, fully connected graph, unweighted updates — **Parley is heterogeneous across four families**, so the theorem does not apply.
- **PARLEY-SIDE (PRIMARY):** Theorem 1 requires confidence to correlate with correctness — i.e. requires agents to grade their own reliability, which line 1259 bans — and requires some voices to count more, which collides with equal signoff weight and line 1294's never-by-counting rule. Parley already has a per-claim epistemic-strength signal in §15 provenance, which is **verifiable rather than self-assessed**.

**Debate or Vote + InfoDelphi** (arXiv:2508.17536, Choi/Zhu/Li, NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight; arXiv:2607.01661)
- **MECHANISM:** Decomposes MAD into voting plus debate and **proves debate is a martingale** under a Dirichlet-Compound-Multinomial belief model with homogeneous, fully-connected agents; the voting supplies the gain. InfoDelphi tests the converse by partitioning evidence into shared-public and disjoint-private subsets so information is transmissible **only** through deliberation.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, citations impeccable) Choi: formal result + 7 NLP benchmarks + Spotlight. InfoDelphi ρ=1.0 ablation verified exactly: accuracy 77.9% → 75.7%, Brier 0.178 → 0.193, with the verbatim conclusion "Removing information asymmetry eliminates most deliberation gains, establishing diversity of input as the key enabler of effective multi-agent reasoning." That is **a single 2.2pp delta on one forecasting benchmark** (PolyGym, 375 binary questions), preprint, no replication.
- **THREE-WAY REFUTATION of the Choi transfer:** (i) Theorem 2's condition is agent **homogeneity** — the paper states it "focuses on homogeneous agent settings, i.e., all agents share the same underlying model architecture", and its Section 6 heterogeneity varies *personas over one base model*. Identical context does not make belief distributions equal, so Parley **violates** the premise rather than satisfying it. (ii) The martingale is over expected correctness against a ground-truth label; Parley's design deliberation has no label, so the conserved quantity is undefined for its main workload. (iii) Choi's positive result is that **majority voting** supplies the gain, and line 1294 structurally forbids resolving by counting — so Parley cannot collect the benefit the paper credits.
- **What stands:** InfoDelphi alone, as a quantified result that **input diversity** rather than talking drives deliberation gains — and the honest tension it names: Parley has zero input asymmetry after round 1, so every later round is maximally homogeneous by construction, and the safety argument for sending everything to everyone is also what flattens later rounds.

**The Selection Bottleneck** (arXiv:2603.20324v1, Maryanskyy, Uber)
- **MECHANISM:** Team oracle O(T), team mean M(T), exploitable gap δ(T) = O(T) − M(T); team held fixed while only aggregation varies across a Bradley-Terry judge panel, majority vote, and MoA-style synthesis.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY, numbers exact: BT-WR 0.810 / 0.512 / 0.496 / 0.179; δ = +0.631 [0.562, 0.696]; Hedges g = 3.86 [3.14, 4.58]) — but **42 tasks total, 6 per category, single author, no replication**, judge-based evaluation scoring judge-based selection, and one of the two "independent" decoupling judges returned ties on 99.6% of 1,260 comparisons. **Disqualifying for the headline:** inter-rater agreement is κ̄ = **0.095** in the diverse+judge condition that produces the 0.810 win rate, κ̄ = 0.667 in the losing homogeneous condition, κ̄ = 0.263 for synthesis. An effect present only where raters agree at chance and absent where they agree well is the standard signature of a measurement artifact.
- **PARLEY-SIDE:** A judge/selector is a design Parley ruled out (equal signoff weight; quorum = all participants; line 1294; §13 replaced single-model self-preference with multi-agent quorum). The MoA→FINAL.md analogy is loose: MoA silently averages competing answers with no objection channel, whereas a FINAL.md drafter is subject to append-only signoff by every participant.

**AgentAuditor — non-voting adjudication at Critical Divergence Points** (arXiv:2602.09341v1, Yang et al., USC)
- **MECHANISM:** Organize N agents' traces into a **Reasoning Tree** (shared prefixes = agreement, branches = divergence), locate **Critical Divergence Points** where trajectories first part, and adjudicate **only there** via localized pairwise branch comparison instead of reading every full trace. ACPO separately trains the adjudicator on historical majority-**failure** cases.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY) Gains ~3% average over majority vote (best +5.7%), ~1–2% over LLM-as-judge — softer than pitched. **The transferable configuration is unmeasured:** the deployed adjudicator requires LoRA fine-tuning (r=16, α=32) on a consensus-trap dataset, and **no reported arm measures the CDP structure with an untrained black-box adjudicator**, which is the only configuration available to Parley's CLI agents. All four benchmarks are single-correct-answer tasks. **Verified transferable number: token cost 1,762 → 973 (44.8% reduction vs LLM-as-Judge)** by auditing only divergence packets.
- **CORRECTION:** The finding's assertion that "Parley has NO positive adjudication procedure at all" is false — §15.3 specifies one ("A resolution MUST explain why the relied-upon evidence applies to and entails the scoped claim, and why contrary sources, checks or counterexamples do not"), and only when that engagement fails does the claim become DISPUTED. **What Parley actually lacks is localization** — PRIMARY: no notion of a divergence point anywhere in COOPERATION.md.
- **Non-collision note:** CDP adjudication selects what the **adjudicator** reads after the fact, from artifacts that stay on disk unmodified. No agent's input is reduced, so no objection can become unrecorded consent.

**Cherry-pick Override** (arXiv:2606.07834v1, Xu, Glasgow) and **Proof-Carrying Numbers** (arXiv:2509.06902, Solatorio, World Bank) — both OVERSTATED, both with a small residue.
- Cherry-pick Override **MECHANISM:** expose CONFLICTING as a first-class non-directional verdict distinct from INSUFFICIENT; separate verdict **generation** from commitment **authorization** in distinct components. **EVIDENCE:** base result robust (>84% directional commitment on gold-CONFLICTING items; VitaminC 77%), but the striking panel-amplification result (0.840→0.887, CI [+0.013,+0.080]) **does not replicate** on the cross-dataset control (CI [−0.060,+0.060]); failure anatomy rests on n=27–28; the authors self-describe as "diagnostic rather than prescriptive" and call the controller "a design probe, not a solution" with improvement CIs straddling zero. **Residue:** Parley's verdict vocabulary has no slot for "evidence sufficient to know no direction can be authorized" as distinct from "not yet verified"; and self-reported confidence measured useless as a filter (86% / 57% / 7% commitment at 0.85 / 0.90 / 0.95 thresholds; ECE 0.07).
- PCN **MECHANISM:** numeric spans emitted as claim-bound tokens tied to a structured claim from an authoritative source; a verifier **in the renderer, not the model**, checks each token; bare untagged numbers are always unverified; soundness, completeness under honest tokens, fail-closed behaviour and monotonicity are **proved**. **EVIDENCE: theorems only — no benchmark, no measured hallucination reduction, no baseline.** PCN verifies that an emitted number matches a **pre-resolved reference value**; it does **not** verify that any computation was performed, has no analogue for "did this agent run `go test`", and binds numbers only, not prose. **What it supports by analogy:** the architectural principle that the verifier must not be the model — which Parley violates, since the agent whose claim is in question assigns its own provenance label (PRIMARY: zero of 201 Go files reference the tags). The rule at lines 1274–1282 is already syntactically checkable; **only enforcement is missing.**

**Can LLM Agents Really Debate?** (arXiv:2511.07784, Wu/Li/Li) — *verdict: OVERSTATED; the finding that collapsed hardest*
- **MECHANISM:** Knight-Knave-Spy puzzles (4–9 players) with verifiable ground truth; **one-factor-at-a-time** sweep around a fixed anchor — seven configurations (A plus C1–C6) at 100 puzzles each, 745 regression observations — plus flip-direction analysis under majority pressure.
- **EVIDENCE:** (SECONDARY) Coefficients verified: depth +0.019 n.s.; agents +0.066 p<0.001; confidence visibility +0.023 n.s.; order +0.035 (**the write-up called this p<0.05; the table marks it not significant**). Models are GPT-5-nano, Qwen-turbo-latest, gemini-2.5-flash, gemini-2.5-flash-lite — small/cheap tier. **Decisive:** debate depth was tested at exactly **two levels** (1 pass vs 2). Parley's measured problem is 1.6→5.1 with a max of 24; the study never enters that regime. "Width beats depth" compares a 3→4 agent step (C1 = "adding the strongest agent", confounding size with strength) against a 1→2 depth step. With ~200 puzzles per contrast, "not significant" is as plausibly low power as a real null. **Omitted dominant coefficient: initial accuracy at +0.600, p<0.001** — an order of magnitude above every structural factor, and the paper's actual result. Asymmetry numbers do hold: 3.6% self-correction for flash-lite vs 34.4% for flash; correct minority positions held above 97%.
- **PARLEY-SIDE:** Parley already scales reviewer width by track (§4: 1 model-diverse reviewer on fast, 2 on standard, all non-implementers on deliberation) and already requires ≥2 independent reviewers before auto-completing (LE-7/LE-11).

## 5. What the literature says AGAINST elaborate multi-agent process

Collected here so it is not buried. Each item is the *negative* claim in its strongest defensible form, with its scope limit attached.

1. **Vanilla debate is a martingale over expected correctness.** Two independent formal results (arXiv:2601.19921 Eq. 14; arXiv:2508.17536 Theorem 2, NeurIPS Spotlight) prove that under their belief models, additional debate rounds do not raise expected correctness; the voting step supplies whatever gain is observed. **Scope: both require agent homogeneity and a ground-truth label. Parley satisfies neither.**
2. **Under compute normalization, most MAD gains disappear.** A 5-framework × 9-benchmark × 4-model sweep of both round count and agent count found "performance often stagnated or declined with more computational resources", and a token-budget-matched study across 6 budget levels found single-agent superiority on multi-hop reasoning, framed by the Data Processing Inequality. **Scope: multi-hop QA and standard benchmarks; the second paper discloses budget-control artifacts and an explicit escape clause where a single agent's context utilization degrades.**
3. **Debate can be actively worse than one model call.** In arXiv:2601.19921's own table, debate alone scores 0.847 against Single Model 0.908 — 6.1pp worse — and the full stacked intervention beats a single call by 2.4pp. In arXiv:2510.20963, competitive debate collapses to 24.89 F2 against ~74–84 single-agent, and consensus-seeking debate (71–76) underperforms simple voting. In arXiv:2605.00914, isolated self-correction beats debate at 2.1–3.4× lower token cost, and a **noise control beat debate** on GSM-Hard (63.2% vs 58.8%) — the authors conceding "task-relevant peer rationales provide no measurable benefit over task-irrelevant ones." **Scope: 7–8B homogeneous swarms running plurality voting.**
4. **Deliberation moves a group away from its best member.** ICML 2026 (arXiv:2602.01011) measured synergy gaps of 6.3%–41.1% against the best individual, growing with team size across all tasks and information conditions (all p<0.05), and surviving every coordination protocol tested — attributed to "integrative compromise", not failure to identify the expert. Even prompts "aggressively optimized… to maximize deference" did not close it.
5. **Most conformity flips make things worse, and vacuous text is enough to cause them.** 63.6% of stance-induced flips are correct→wrong (57–77% across families), and argument-shaped-but-contentless peer text drove 20–39% error adoption among agents that had resisted the bare position.
6. **Procedural excellence does not imply epistemic quality.** 1,980 five-agent deliberations achieved human-level discourse quality (2.939 vs 2.980) while delivering ~30% of the human outcome gain (ΔDRI 0.029 vs 0.099), and diverged in perspective where humans converged.
7. **Facts attrit across deliberation rounds.** Critical-fact retention fell 79.0 / 60.1 / 46.5 at system level and 35.0 / 24.7 / 18.3 at agent level over three rounds.
8. **Adding process has a measured ceiling.** The best-evidenced interventions in the corpus bought +9.4% and +15.6%, and the peer-reviewed authors state plainly that "task completion rates still remain low, indicating that more substantial improvements are needed."
9. **Verify-repair loops with noisy verifiers destroy correct work.** 55% of instances saw a correct plan repaired into an incorrect one; 24% of those damaging repairs won majority acceptance; acceptance can rise monotonically while true validity collapses (0.87 at round 2 → 0.12 at round 6).
10. **Agent code review, at real scale, produces mostly non-actionable commentary.** 60.2% of closed agent-only PRs fall in the 0–30% signal range; 12 of 13 agents average below 60%.
11. **Vendor guidance says Parley's shape is the bad fit.** Anthropic: multi-agent pays on parallel breadth-first search; "domains that require all agents to share the same context or involve many dependencies between agents are not a good fit for multi-agent systems today"; "For sequential tasks, same-file edits, or work with many dependencies, a single session or subagents are more effective."
12. **The one paper that measures architectural bloat measures the wrong object.** arXiv:2606.13003's "architectural bloat that prioritizes superficial complexity which does not translate into functional utility" describes **automatically generated** architectures, and its own positive result is that expert-architected systems consistently beat them.

**Two facts that bound how far items 1–12 can be pushed.** First, **not one of these sources measures multi-turn software design deliberation or repository code review** — the only workload Parley has. Second, the three findings that pressed hardest toward "shrink the coordination machinery" were each traced by a verifier to a distinct transfer error (a single-agent-dominated workload where inter-agent modes cannot fire; a homogeneity theorem Parley violates; a paper about auto-generated architectures) — so their agreement is not corroboration.

## 6. Where Parley Deck appears to be ahead

Stated as facts with the check that establishes each. This section is not flattery; two of these items were established by verifiers *trying and failing* to find a gap.

1. **Provenance grading is better founded than the literature's confidence signals.** Papers that need an epistemic-strength signal use self-reported confidence, which arXiv:2606.07834 measures as a useless filter (ECE 0.07; 86%/57%/7% commitment at rising thresholds) and which the confidence-weighting theorem requires to correlate with correctness. Parley's PRIMARY/SECONDARY/RECALL is verifiable rather than self-assessed, and provenance **caps** the verdict (lines 1274–1282). (PRIMARY for the deck side.)
2. **Never resolving by count, including unanimous count.** Line 1294. The corpus's most damaging results — Bleichenbacher unanimity across 10 agents, correct-to-wrong conformity, consensus inflation, the 32.3pp oracle gap, self-preference bias — are all failures of counting. Parley's DISPUTED machinery (lines 1299–1305) additionally blocks a DISPUTED claim from supporting any acceptance criterion. (PRIMARY.)
3. **Correlated-agreement awareness is already in the protocol.** Lines 1356–1360 bind `consensus.md` to record that "unanimity among related models is a shared prior, not independent evidence" and require FINAL.md to state where nominally independent proposals are one family. The self-preference-bias paper (COLM 2026, the only peer-reviewed source in its lens) motivates exactly this and was graded ALREADY-HAVE. (PRIMARY.)
4. **No self-verdicts.** Line 1259. Motivated independently by the self-preference literature (GPT-5 HSPP-Rubric ratio 20.15; "more than 50% more likely" to overestimate its own output on LiveCodeBench). (PRIMARY.)
5. **Append-only, single-writer, no summarization step, nothing dropped.** The mitigation the "Deliberative Illusion" paper actually recommends — "shared evidence ledgers" and "fact-level memory" — is a description of this design. ConstraintRot's mitigation ("pin the governance class") is implemented here in its maximal form: PRIMARY, "compaction" appears once in 105,382 bytes, as the thing files survive.
6. **Cold-start independence with anchoring named as the rationale.** Line 324. Refute-or-Promote treats this as a novel contribution; Parley shipped it. (PRIMARY.)
7. **Trajectory-based stopping rather than fixed-K.** Lines 646–666, including "Hitting the budget never marks an implementation complete." Two separate findings assumed Parley used fixed caps; both were wrong. (PRIMARY.)
8. **A recorded concession mechanism and a purpose-built minority escalation.** Lines 552–556 and line 690 — the latter is a direct structural answer to the CosMAD failure mode, weakened only by being voluntary and self-triggered. (PRIMARY.)
9. **Ephemeral vs canonical channel separation, stricter than the A2A standard.** Line 802 vs A2A's "Messages MUST NOT be considered a reliable delivery mechanism for critical information". (SECONDARY for A2A, PRIMARY for line 802.)
10. **A positive per-round acknowledgment receipt** (`driver.go:363-372` requires `responding-to` frontmatter plus a per-agent `### @<other>` heading from round 2), which most surveyed frameworks lack.
11. **Same-artifact write conflict is structurally impossible.** Claude Code's agent-teams documentation answers this hazard with a best-practice line telling the human to partition files, with overwrite as the failure mode; Parley's one-file-per-agent rule removes the class.
12. **The completeness standard itself is stricter than the entire surveyed industry.** OpenAI's Agents SDK handoff filters, LangGraph's default reducer (which silently discards one of two concurrent writes), and MetaGPT's role-profile subscription all ship context selection with **no preservation guarantee** — and MetaGPT's subscription mechanism, the field's most-cited instance, **has never been ablated** (its two published ablations isolate roles and executable feedback, not publish-subscribe). Even the best-in-class statistical instance (conformal filtering, ECIR 2026) offers only a **marginal, population-level** coverage bound, not a per-run guarantee.
13. **Hand-architected rather than auto-generated** — the property arXiv:2606.13003 measures as the one that wins.
14. **A machine-enforced verifier-type asymmetry already exists** (line 592, LE-4: the driver "can only fail a close claim, never auto-pass one"), plus a machine-enforced `## Refutation attempts` gate (`phase58.go:437`), plus round-artifact and cross-review validators.
15. **Roster authority as a generated, explicitly non-authoritative view** (line 1094) — the same move FORGE makes at policy level, already working at small scale in this system.

## 9. What nobody measured

- No source measures **protocol-rule compliance as a function of protocol length**. The instruction-density literature tops out at 500 keyword instructions (string-matched) or depth 20; COOPERATION.md is 105,382 bytes of conditional, cross-referencing procedural prose carrying hundreds of rules. Every application to Parley is extrapolation beyond the measured range.
- No source measures **multi-turn software design deliberation** or **repository-scale code review by frontier agents**. One source measures real PRs, and it measures whether comments were acted on, not whether they were correct.
- No source measures the **artifact-reference primitive** in isolation (the 90.2% figure covers a whole system), the **CDP structure with an untrained adjudicator**, the **adversarial kill mandate** in isolation (explicitly no ablation), **MetaGPT's publish-subscribe subscription**, or **fix-up budget split by verifier type** (proposed by a researcher, tested by neither cited paper).
- No source establishes a **no-drop guarantee**. The canary/tap instrument detects loss at deterministic boundaries; the paper formalizes no guarantee, and once an objection must survive **attention** rather than a pipe, canaries detect loss without preventing it.
- Parley itself has never measured: acted-on fraction of findings per review round; consensus level vs finding severity per round; DC-vs-DM classification of fix-up cycles; run-to-run reproducibility (Pass²) of the same idea; outcome quality normalized by token or wall-clock spend; a compute-matched single-agent control; what the 1.6→5.1 series decomposes into (agent error vs harness/protocol-forced re-run); or what the always-send-everything rule buys.
