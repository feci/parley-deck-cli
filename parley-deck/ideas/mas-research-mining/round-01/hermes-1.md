## Summary

After reading the full brief (347 lines) and verifying the deck-side locators it cites against this repository, my honest conclusion is that this protocol should do LESS new mechanism-building and MORE measurement of what it already runs. The brief's §6 lists fifteen places where Parley is ahead of the surveyed field, and I confirmed the load-bearing ones in source: provenance caps verdicts (COOPERATION.md:1274-1282), no self-verdicts (:1259), never-resolve-by-count (:1294), trajectory-based stopping (:646-666), cold-start independence at round 1 (:324), and a recorded concession mechanism in review (:552-556). The verifiers tried and failed to find gaps in several of these. Meanwhile §5 collects twelve results against elaborate multi-agent process, and §9 lists what nobody measured — including Parley itself.

I return THREE proposals, ranked. The first is an instrumentation proposal that touches no protocol bytes and addresses T1 directly. The second is a minimal review-round signal that addresses T4 (the VRR-Stop / DM problem) and costs ~0 net bytes. The third is a §7 successor-idea shape for a document-wide precedence order, addressing T5, explicitly deferred to a successor rather than proposed here. I reject T2's framing as a proposal-generating tension and explain why. T3 I judge to be a feature, not a bug, and say so.

The common thread: every proposal attacks the measured explosion in review/fix-up rounds (1.6→5.1, max 24), not the flat design phase. Every proposal names what evidence would falsify it. And every proposal either adds zero protocol bytes or explicitly routes through §7.

## Proposed approach

### P1 (RANK 1) — Review-round instrumentation: DC/DM decomposition and acted-on fraction, built from data already on disk

MECHANISM. The driver already does a deterministic finding-scan (`internal/driver/impl.go:414` `scanHasRealFinding`) that counts severity-tagged headings in review files. It counts whether findings exist, not what kind they are. The Detection-Without-Correction paper (arXiv:2605.27559, SECONDARY — I did not read it) decomposes each round-to-round transition into DC (detect-correct) and DM (detect-mis-correct), and reports P(DM|D=1) of 53-94% across 14 cohorts. The brief notes (§4.2, PRIMARY) that "a fix that gets re-BLOCKed on the same claim is an observed DM and is currently never counted." Separately, the AIDev/PR-review study (§4.2, SECONDARY) shows that computing acted-on fraction of findings per round is label-free and Parley already records dispositions but never computes the ratio.

The proposal: extend the existing finding-scan to classify each fix-up transition as DC or DM by matching a round-N+1 finding to the round-N finding it claims to address (the review consensus already cites originating findings — COOPERATION.md:571 "from <agent-id>/review/round-01 [MAJOR] <short-title>"). A re-block on the same claim is a DM. The acted-on fraction is (findings that became Agreed fixes) / (total findings that round). Both are computed from artifacts already on disk. No new dependency, no new service, no tool in the normative path — this is a read-only analysis pass over review directories, analogous to what `parley retro` already does conceptually (COOPERATION.md:1189 references retro tooling as read-only).

THE MEASURED PARLEY PROBLEM IT TOUCHES. The 1.6→5.1 review-round explosion (max 24). If a substantial fraction of fix-up cycles are DM (mis-corrections that re-block), that is a structural explanation for why rounds explode: the loop is not converging, it is thrashing. The MAST production-telemetry residue (§7, dropped but with a methodological note) flags that Parley's 5.1-round figure has never been decomposed into "agent was wrong" vs "harness/protocol forced a re-run" — this instrumentation answers that.

WHAT PARLEY ALREADY HAS. The finding-scan exists (`impl.go:414`). The disposition mechanism exists (:546-550). The review consensus cites originating findings (:571). The trajectory-stopping heuristic exists (:656-659) and defines "converging" as "total findings dropping sharply each pass" — but this is a raw count, not a DC/DM decomposition. The protocol has no notion of a mis-correction signal.

CONCRETE SHAPE. A `parley retro --fixup-analysis <idea-slug>` subcommand (or extending the existing retro scaffolding) that, for each review cycle in a completed idea, reports: findings per round, acted-on fraction, DC count, DM count, and whether any DM was re-blocked on the same claim in a subsequent round. This is a read-only diagnostic. It does not change the protocol. It does not gate anything. It gives the operator and the retrospective (§13) the data to judge whether the trajectory-stopping heuristic is being fooled by DM thrashing — which is exactly what VRR-Stop (§4.2, SECONDARY) predicts: acceptance (finding count dropping) can rise while true validity falls.

COST OF BEING WRONG. Low. The instrumentation is read-only and diagnostic. If the DC/DM decomposition is noisy or the matching heuristic is imprecise, the worst case is a misleading retrospective metric that the operator ignores. It does not change any gate, does not drop any objection, does not reduce any agent's input.

WHAT EVIDENCE WOULD SHOW IT DID NOT WORK. Run the analysis over the completed ideas in this repo. If the DM fraction is negligible (say <10% of fix-up transitions) across multiple ideas, then mis-correction is not the driver of the 5.1-round explosion, and the hypothesis is falsified — the explosion is something else (harness-forced re-runs, genuine new defects, or protocol structure). If the acted-on fraction is uniformly high (>80%), then reviewers are not producing noise and the review loop is earning its rounds. Either way the measurement is valuable, but the specific "DM thrashing explains the explosion" hypothesis would be dead.

### P2 (RANK 2) — A re-block signal in the trajectory-stopping heuristic, costing ~0 net protocol bytes

MECHANISM. COOPERATION.md:656-659 defines "churning" as "the finding count holding steady over two passes, or new CRITICAL/MAJOR findings on previously unchanged code." VRR-Stop (§4.2, SECONDARY) proves that a converging acceptance signal (findings dropping) can rise while true validity collapses. The sharper framing from the brief: :657-659 defines converging as "total findings dropping sharply each pass," which is precisely the acceptance signal shown to be unreliable. The DM decomposition from P1 gives us the missing signal: a finding that is re-blocked on the same claim after a fix-up is an observed mis-correction. If two consecutive fix-up rounds produce DM on the same claim, that is churning by a stronger definition than "finding count holding steady."

The proposal: add one clause to the §6 stopping-judgment illustrative triggers. Current text at :658-659: churning looks like "the finding count holding steady over two passes, or new CRITICAL/MAJOR findings on previously unchanged code." Add: "or the same claim re-blocked after a fix-up in two consecutive passes." This is a ~15-word addition to illustrative examples (explicitly non-normative per :656 "examples, not normative thresholds"). It gives the trajectory heuristic a signal that VRR-Stop proves the raw count cannot provide.

THE MEASURED PARLEY PROBLEM IT TOUCHES. The same 1.6→5.1 explosion. If the trajectory heuristic currently cannot distinguish "findings dropping because fixes worked" from "findings dropping because the fix introduced a new defect that hasn't been found yet" (the VRR-Stop collapse), then a re-block signal is the cheapest possible patch. It does not require a belief state, a false-accept/reject estimate, or a damage term β — it requires only the matching that P1's instrumentation already does.

WHAT PARLEY ALREADY HAS. The trajectory heuristic (:646-666). The re-block itself is already visible in the artifacts — a reviewer re-raising a finding cites the prior disposition (:546-550). The piece that is missing is treating a re-block as a churning signal rather than just another finding.

CONCRETE SHAPE. This is a COOPERATION.md edit, so it is a §7 successor idea. The successor idea proposes: (a) build P1's instrumentation, (b) add the re-block clause to :658-659, (c) run P1 over historical ideas to calibrate whether re-block frequency correlates with long fix-up series. The byte cost is ~15 words × 3 lockstep copies = ~45 words net. This is small enough that it does not require a compensating removal, but the successor idea should name one if the operator demands net-zero.

COST OF BEING WRONG. Moderate. If the re-block signal fires too aggressively, it could cause premature escalation on ideas where a legitimate fix takes two attempts for non-DM reasons (e.g., the first fix was incomplete but directionally correct). But the clause only adds a churning trigger — it does not change the close criteria, and :661-663 already says hitting the budget escalates rather than closes. The risk is escalation fatigue, not false completion.

WHAT EVIDENCE WOULD SHOW IT DID NOT WORK. After implementation, run ideas that previously hit long fix-up series (5+ rounds) and check whether the re-block signal would have fired earlier than the actual escalation. If the signal fires at the same time as the existing trajectory heuristic already escalates, it adds no information. If it fires earlier and the earlier escalation would have prevented downstream DM damage, that is evidence it works. If it fires earlier but the downstream rounds were actually productive DC (genuine new defects, not mis-corrections), then the signal is producing false churning positives and should be withdrawn.

### P3 (RANK 3, §7 SUCCESSOR) — A document-wide precedence order across the 18 sections

MECHANISM. COOPERATION.md has 18 top-level sections (verified: `grep -c '^## '` = 18) with no document-wide priority order. §15 has already had to declare pairwise subordination twice (:1235 "composes with §4.0's per-track table and never overrides it"; :1322 "P6 governs that, and this section never overrides it"). WIRE (§4.1, SECONDARY) finds that within-policy rule collisions are resolved silently 64.6% of the time, with no signal that a conflict occurred. Claude's Constitution (§4.1, SECONDARY) handles this with one document-wide priority order. The brief notes Parley already makes this move at small scale — roster authority in `agents.toml` with a generated, non-authoritative §2 view (:1094).

The proposal is a §7 successor idea that adds a single precedence-order statement to §1 (Scope and purpose), naming the resolution order for cross-section conflicts. This is not a new mechanism; it is a meta-rule that makes the existing pairwise overrides systematic rather than ad hoc.

THE MEASURED PARLEY PROBLEM IT TOUCHES. T5 frames this as rule count and collisions. The measured problem is protocol size (105,382 bytes, +112% in ten weeks) and the instruction-stacking collapse result (§4.1, SECONDARY: depth-20 follow rates 60.4%/43.3%/20.1%, degrading by silent omission). But the brief's own scope limit is sharp: the instruction-density literature tops out at 500 keyword instructions or depth 20, while COOPERATION.md is conditional cross-referencing prose with hundreds of rules — "every application to Parley is extrapolation beyond the measured range." And the negative half of the stacking result says rewriting tighter recovers compliance only for weak models (+11.0pp/+3.3pp/−1.2pp; ρ=−0.85). So the precedence order is not justified by the compliance literature. It is justified by the narrower, verified observation that Parley already does pairwise subordination ad hoc and that WIRE's silent-collision finding is a real failure mode even if its transfer to Parley is an analogy.

WHAT PARLEY ALREADY HAS. Pairwise override clauses scattered across sections. No global order. The tier mechanism exists for playbooks (:1187, advisory and non-canonical) but has never been applied to the protocol's own sections.

CONCRETE SHAPE. A §7 successor idea proposing: add 3-5 lines to §1 establishing a precedence order (e.g., §15 verification integrity > §4.0 track gates > §5 quorum > §6 conflict-avoidance > all other sections, or whatever ordering the successor idea argues for). The byte cost is ~3-5 lines × 3 = ~9-15 lines net. The successor idea must argue the specific ordering and must run the `meta-protocol-change-subtractive-maintenance` rule inventory before and after.

COST OF BEING WRONG. The ordering could be wrong — a precedence order that subordinates a section that should be co-equal could silently suppress a valid objection path. But the current state (ad hoc pairwise overrides) already has this risk; a global order makes it explicit and reviewable rather than implicit and scattered.

WHAT EVIDENCE WOULD SHOW IT DID NOT WORK. After ratification, track whether cross-section conflicts that previously went silent (if any were observed) now produce a resolvable signal. If no conflicts are ever observed in practice (the 0.55% WIRE yield suggests collisions are rare), then the precedence order is dead code — harmless but load-bearing for nothing. If conflicts are observed and the order resolves them in the direction the protocol intends, it works. If conflicts are observed and the order resolves them wrongly (a section that should have won loses), the ordering needs revision. The key falsifiable prediction: the order will be invoked. If it is never invoked across N completed ideas, it is confirmed as unnecessary weight.

## Concerns / open questions

### T2 — Asymmetric context: I reject the framing as a proposal generator

T2 observes four lines converging on "give reviewers less context, not designers": Anthropic's verification-needs-minimal-context claim (§4.4, weakest evidence tier — vendor self-report, no measurement behind the artifact-reference primitive), Refute-or-Promote's cold-start reviewers (§4.2, the adversarial-framing claim was refuted; only the execution gate survived), ICML's teams-hold-experts-back (§7, dropped as ALREADY-HAVE — lines 1294 and 1274-1282 already let a lone PRIMARY beat a unanimous RECALL group), and Parley's own 1.6-vs-5.1 split.

The convergence is real but the brief itself flags the critical problem: "this sits exactly where the deleted context selector sat." The `cognee-mechanism-mining` standing conclusion (00-prompt.md:108-111) is binding: a mechanism that decides what an agent sees must prove it never drops an objection. The context-asymmetry proposal cannot meet that bar — it is structurally a context-selection mechanism. The four converging lines are each individually weak (the brief says so), and three of the four are either refuted on their Parley mapping or already absorbed. The fourth (the 1.6-vs-5.1 split) is an observation, not a mechanism.

My check: COOPERATION.md:324 confirms cold-start independence at design round 1. The review phase (Phase 6) does not enforce it. But the reason design round 1 is flat (1.6) and review explodes (5.1) is not established by the brief as a context-asymmetry effect — it is an unexplained correlation. The `gatherPriorRounds` function (runner.go:938) sends all prior rounds to all agents in all phases, so the context volume is the same mechanism in both phases. The difference is that design round 1 has no prior rounds to send (it is round 1), while review rounds accumulate. This is a confound: the flat design phase may be flat because it is round 1 (no context to be asymmetric about), not because independence is enforced.

I do not propose a context-asymmetry mechanism. The honest conclusion is that the correlation is under-specified and the mechanism is barred by a standing conclusion. If the facilitator wants to pursue this, the first step is P1-style instrumentation: measure whether review rounds with more prior-round context produce more findings (which would support the asymmetry hypothesis) or fewer (which would support the "more context = better" default). Until that measurement exists, proposing a context-reduction mechanism is building on a correlation that has not been distinguished from a confound.

### T3 — The ungated finding channel is a feature, not a bug

T3 asks whether the ungated finding channel (§15.4, :1321 — provenance gates entry to consensus.md, not what a reviewer may report) is a feature protecting genuine dissent or the cost driver. My judgment: it is a feature, and the brief's own evidence supports this.

The brief notes (:1321, PRIMARY) that §15.4 "deliberately does not gate what a reviewer may report." The disjunction in LE-1 (COOPERATION.md:254 — "attempt a failing case or run the relevant check") governs reviewer diligence, not finding admissibility. A CRITICAL asserted from RECALL can open a fix-up cycle today. But the verdict-cap machinery (:1274-1282) means that RECALL-tagged finding reaches consensus.md as UNVERIFIED, not as CONFIRMED. The finding can open a review cycle (which costs rounds) but cannot close a gate (which requires CONFIRMED/WRONG from PRIMARY or at least SECONDARY).

The cost of being wrong about this: if we gate findings by provenance, we suppress the exact case the protocol is designed to protect — a lone agent with a correct objection but only RECALL-level evidence. Lines 1294 and 1274-1282 already give that agent structural priority over a unanimous RECALL group. Gating the finding channel would reverse that. The brief's §6.2 identifies never-resolving-by-count as a place Parley is ahead, and the Bleichenbacher unanimity anecdote (§4.2, SECONDARY — 10 agents unanimously confirmed a non-existent vulnerability) is the canonical example of why gating by authority rather than evidence is dangerous.

The real question is whether the fix-up cycles opened by RECALL-level CRITICALs are a substantial fraction of the 5.1-round explosion. That is measurable (P1), and until it is measured, gating the channel is a solution to an unmeasured problem that risks the protocol's core dissent-protection design.

### T5 — The trap in the framing

T5's framing contains its own warning, and I want to make it explicit. The instruction-stacking result (§4.1, SECONDARY) says that rewriting the instruction set tighter recovers compliance only for weak models and is negative for strong models (−1.2pp for the strongest). The Spearman correlation is ρ=−0.85 (p=0.004, n=9). This means the instinctive fix — tighten the protocol text — is empirically predicted to make compliance worse for the frontier models Parley runs. P3 (precedence order) is not a tightening; it is a meta-rule that resolves ambiguity. But any successor idea that proposes to "clean up" or "tighten" the protocol text should be viewed against this result. The `meta-protocol-change-subtractive-maintenance` idea (00-prompt.md:127-128) already requires a mechanical before/after rule inventory, which is the right discipline. The standing risk is that the subtraction framing becomes an excuse to rewrite, and rewriting is the intervention the evidence says does not work on strong models.

### What I considered and rejected

- ARC compaction (§4.4): the brief notes Parley agents can already read any round file on demand, so "recall" informally exists. The genuinely missing piece is an audit log of what was and was not opened. But this is a context-selection mechanism and is barred by the cognee standing conclusion unless it proves non-drop. I do not see how to prove that an agent that did not issue a recall command did not need the recalled item. Rejected.

- Confidence-weighted aggregation (§4.6): the theorem requires agents to grade their own reliability, which COOPERATION.md:1259 bans, and requires some voices to count more, which :1294 forbids. Already-ruled-out. Rejected.

- CDP localization (§4.6, AgentAuditor): the transferable configuration (untrained black-box adjudicator at divergence points) is unmeasured. The adjudication procedure already exists (§15.3). The token-cost reduction (44.8%) is real but addresses cost, not the round-count explosion. Rejected as not touching a measured problem.

- A "Partially supported" verdict (§4.3, FactReview): the brief notes only this is genuinely missing from Parley's verdict vocabulary. But it is not clear what measured problem this solves. Adding a verdict category without a measured need is speculative. Rejected.

## Risks

1. P1 and P2 are coupled — P2's re-block signal depends on P1's DC/DM matching. If P1's matching heuristic is imprecise (e.g., it matches findings by title similarity rather than claim identity), P2's signal inherits the noise. The successor idea should specify the matching strategy and test it on historical ideas before proposing the protocol change.

2. All three proposals are ultimately about the review/fix-up loop, which is the right term (1.6→5.1, max 24). But none of them addresses the root question the brief raises in T1: is the 5.1-round figure itself compute-confounded? If every historical Parley claim about more rounds helping is confounded by +112% protocol growth and 3.3× wall clock, then the 5.1 figure might be a symptom of protocol bloat, not of a fix-up loop pathology. P1's instrumentation would help answer this (by correlating round count with protocol version / byte count across historical ideas), but I cannot make that determination without the data, and the brief does not provide it.

3. P3 (precedence order) is the weakest proposal. The WIRE collision yield is 0.55% (SECONDARY), and the transfer to Parley is an analogy. The brief's §3.1 explicitly warns that duplicate sources across lenses are not corroboration, and WIRE was surfaced by multiple lenses. The precedence order may be a solution to a problem that is rare in practice. I rank it third because it is low-cost and makes existing ad hoc overrides systematic, but it is the proposal most likely to be confirmed as unnecessary.

4. I did not read any of the ~30 cited papers. Every external-research claim in this file is SECONDARY at best, per the brief's own provenance convention (§0). The DC/DM numbers, the VRR-Stop collapse, the WIRE collision rate, and the instruction-stacking compliance figures are all as reported by the brief's verifier passes, not as I read them in the sources. If the brief contains an error I did not catch, my proposals inherit it.
